## 3. A British healthcare worker who tested positive for Ebola in the Democratic Republic of Congo is to be flown back to the UK for treatment, with the public risk said to remain low

A UK government spokesperson said there were "well-established procedures for dealing with cases like this" and that the risk to the public remains low.

The Guardian said the patient, a healthcare worker sent by a humanitarian organisation, is in a stable condition and was expected to arrive in the UK on Wednesday or Thursday before transfer by specialist ambulance to a high-consequence infectious disease unit, according to an official document seen by Reuters. Imported Ebola cases are extremely rare in the UK and have resulted in no deaths, the BBC said, with only four confirmed UK cases historically.

Sky News quoted an expert describing the case as "concerning, but not entirely unexpected". The current outbreak, declared in May and driven by the Bundibugyo variant, has surpassed 10,000 cases and become the deadliest in DR Congo's history, with a reported fatality rate near 49%. There is no approved vaccine for this variant, though WHO-backed trial candidates are being prepared.

## 5. More asbestos is found in Parliament as MPs face a crunch decision on a restoration that could cost tens of billions and take decades

More asbestos has been discovered in Parliament, the BBC reported, as MPs come under pressure to sign off on a major revamp of the crumbling Palace of Westminster. Known asbestos items on the parliamentary estate now number more than 1,650, an increase of 500 since 2021, after fresh surveys for the long-delayed restoration project. The government is considering a vote on a refurbishment that could cost up to £40bn and take 61 years.

The Commons public accounts committee this week warned it was "crunch time" for the restoration, with every additional year of delay costing taxpayers £420m, the BBC said. In the past five years 700 asbestos items have been removed, but the total has still risen. Most is classed as low risk in concealed areas, though almost 200 items are medium risk. Parliamentary authorities said safety of those who work on and visit the estate is the absolute priority.

The Times has warned that Parliament risks a Notre Dame-style fire without a £16bn restoration, a line France 24 also carried for international readers. Kris Gledhill, whose mother, former Labour MP Alice Mahon, died of mesothelioma, told the BBC asbestos should never be considered low risk. Authorities said an asbestos-free building cannot be achieved in practice without demolition or extensive damage to the fabric.

## 7. Hong Kong democracy activist Joshua Wong is sentenced to 10 years in jail for colluding with foreign forces under the national security law

Joshua Wong, once the face of Hong Kong's democracy movement, has been sentenced to 10 years in jail for colluding with foreign forces under the city's national security law, the BBC reported. Already imprisoned for subversion and due to be freed in January, Wong, who turned 30 last month, will now remain behind bars until around the end of 2036, with part of the new term served concurrently.
