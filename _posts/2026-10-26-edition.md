## 1. Two men are killed by falling trees as Storm Boelo leaves tens of thousands of homes without power

Two men were killed by falling trees on Sunday as Storm Boelo brought severe weather to the UK, the BBC reported. A man in his 20s died in Finchley, north London, after a tree fell onto the car he was driving on Dollis Road at about 12:40 GMT, the Metropolitan Police said. Hours earlier, Police Scotland said a 60-year-old man died after being hit by a falling tree at Mid Kelton near Castle Douglas in Dumfries and Galloway. Two women were taken to hospital after another tree fell in Aberfeldy.

More than 90,000 properties were without power at 19:00 GMT on Sunday, the Energy Networks Association said, adding that dangerous conditions were limiting engineers' chances to start repairs safely. Across Storm Boelo and the earlier Storm Austen, which hit on Friday, 838,700 properties faced power cuts. Gusts peaked at 85mph in Machrihanish in Argyll and Bute and 84mph in Stornoway and Capel Curig. Amber and yellow Met Office wind warnings expired at 21:00 on Sunday, and flood warnings were issued for parts of England and Wales. The Daily Mail reported that more wild weather could follow, while Al Jazeera said the two storms also cut power in France and Germany.

## 3. Funding to end rough sleeping in England is set to double to almost £900m as Andy Burnham hosts a Downing Street summit

Funding is set to double for Prime Minister Andy Burnham's pledge to give every rough sleeper in England a route off the streets by Christmas, the BBC reported. Ending rough sleeping was one of his first promises when he entered Downing Street, with a £442m package for emergency accommodation and support. On the eve of the first Rough Sleeping Summit at Downing Street on Monday, No 10 indicated a further £432m will be added in Wednesday's Budget, taking total investment to £874m and extending support beyond March 2027 to the end of this Parliament.

The BBC understands the commitment is that every rough sleeper will be able to move into accommodation by Christmas or be offered support. Outreach teams and new bed spaces are operating in Coventry, with provision rolling out in Plymouth and Camden. More than 30 organisations have pledged support, and the National Housing Federation is working to identify at least 2,000 potential homes and bed spaces. "Rough sleeping isn't a fact of life," Burnham said. Shadow housing secretary Katie Lam said rough sleeping had hit "record highs under Labour" and criticised his record as mayor of Greater Manchester. The Guardian described the total as almost £900m.
