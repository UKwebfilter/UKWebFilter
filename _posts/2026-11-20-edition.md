## 1. Four men are convicted of sexually abusing Joanne Young, who was drugged by her husband and offered to strangers, as she calls for the law on consent to change

Four men have been convicted of sexually abusing Joanne Young, a woman who was drugged by her then husband and advertised for sex online, in what the Crown Prosecution Service said was the first time a jury had convicted multiple defendants over organised drug-facilitated sexual assault in England. Connor Sanderson-Doyle, 32, Richard Wilkins, 62, Norman Macksoni, 48, and Mohammed Hassan, 38, were found guilty of various offences after a three-week trial at Winchester Crown Court.

Philip Young, 50, a former Conservative councillor, had already pleaded guilty to dozens of charges of drugging and raping his wife over a 13-year period at their home in Swindon. The court heard that he sedated her into an "inanimate state" and invited men he met through websites and apps. Joanne Young, who has waived her right to anonymity, said: "The person I trusted most in the world betrayed me in the most horrific way." Outside court she said consent "has to come from the person themselves" and urged the government to treat the law as a priority. Wiltshire Police said drug-facilitated sexual offences could be significantly underreported, and the Guardian reported that officers had taken advice from the investigators in the Gisèle Pelicot case in France.

The verdicts led Friday's Daily Mail ("The bravest woman in Britain"), Daily Express ("brave beyond all measure") and Daily Mirror. Every UK outlet we track carried the story, and it was picked up by the New York Times, Washington Post, CNN, France 24, Reuters and AP, several of which drew the comparison with the Pelicot case.

## 2. The UK and EU are set to agree a youth mobility scheme for up to 100,000 people a year, as negotiators race to finish a reset deal before Friday's Brussels summit

The UK and the European Union are expected to agree a youth mobility scheme at a summit in Brussels on Friday that would allow up to 100,000 young people from the EU to travel, work and study in the UK each year, the BBC reported. The same number of young Britons would be able to go to Europe. According to the Guardian, the UK raised its proposed cap from between 50,000 and 80,000 to 100,000 and agreed to lift the upper age limit from 30 to 31.

The Guardian said negotiators were closing in on a deal late on Thursday after the EU dropped a demand that EU students pay home tuition fees at British universities, with a supplementary agreement to settle details within six months. A dispute over how far the UK could shape future EU food and farming rules ended with Britain to be consulted rather than given a vote. Downing Street said talks were going "down to the wire".

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he would walk away if necessary but pointed to "lower food prices", lower costs for business and a possible "Channel action plan" on immigration. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch accused him of "reopening the divisions" of the Brexit referendum. The Daily Mail called the deal "the start of EU surrender" and ran a leader on "the shame" of it, and the Sun said the prime minister was "all yap and no bite". CNN, the Washington Post, Al Jazeera and France 24 previewed the summit.

## 3. Donald Trump tells European allies to stop funding Ukraine after his Shenzhen summit with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping, as the three leaders issue a 'peace declaration' that leaves out Ukraine and Iran

The three issued a "Declaration toward Peace" saying "a nuclear war cannot be won and must not be fought" and promising to boost oil supplies, but, as the Guardian and ABC News noted, it made no mention of the wars in Ukraine or Iran.

At the Berlin meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Europe had "a clear and consistent and united negotiating position", and Germany's Friedrich Merz said Moscow's "maximalist demands are not realistic". US envoy Jared Kushner said Trump wanted a deal "with Europe by his side" and that Russia was now "comfortable" with Nato-style security guarantees for Ukraine. Putin said Russia had invited Europeans to Moscow to set out their positions. CGTN published the declaration in full, and TASS quoted Putin as saying Russia was trying to end the war, not start it.

## 4. A Florida judge rules that Thomas Markle, the Duchess of Sussex's father, is mentally fit to stand trial on charges of possessing child sexual abuse images

A judge in Florida has ruled that Thomas Markle, the 82-year-old father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, is mentally competent to stand trial on charges of possessing child sexual abuse material. The finding followed an evaluation by a court-appointed psychiatrist, Dr Amie Gerodimos, and his defence lawyer said it would not be contested. Markle has denied the charges.

The FBI arrested Markle on 6 November at a senior care centre in Lakeland, Florida. According to the Guardian, investigators seized about 78 items, including slides, film negatives and books said to contain suspected abuse images, from two storage units he had rented in the Los Angeles area since 2008, and the FBI said some images appear to show him with children. He was initially released on bond but was jailed again after he was accused of cutting off his electronic ankle monitor on 10 November. The case is in federal court in Tampa and is expected to move to southern California. Police in the Philippines, where he previously lived, have also filed a criminal complaint.

The ruling was widely reported in the UK. The Daily Mail said Markle claimed during the evaluation that he had only "one daughter", while the Telegraph reported that he had "called Meghan his sister" in the test. The ruling came during the Sussexes' visit to Britain, as pictures of Harry and Meghan with their children at Winter Wonderland in Hyde Park appeared in most of Friday's papers. The Sun, which had reported how the images came to light, and Reuters also carried the ruling.

## 5. Grand Theft Auto 6 becomes the biggest entertainment launch on record, with estimated sales of $2.36bn, as fans around the world skip work and school to play it

Coverage also turned to the game's content.
