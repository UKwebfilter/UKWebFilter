## 3. Typical household energy bills are forecast to rise by £443 a year, or 26%, from January, as inflation hits a 12-month high

Gas and electricity bills for a typical household are forecast to jump by £443 a year from January, a 26% rise that would take a typical annual bill to £2,166, according to consultancy Cornwall Insight. The official price cap figure is due next week. Several newspapers made the forecast a front-page story, with Metro leading on "Boiling point" and the Daily Mirror calling it a blow to vulnerable families and pensioners.

The BBC said inflation rose to 3.6% in the year to October, the highest rate in a year, driven mainly by petrol and diesel prices. It said the US-Israel war with Iran has pushed up the cost of energy and fuel in recent months, and that the Bank of England this month raised interest rates from 3.75% to 4%, the first increase in three years, and signalled a further rise could follow unless the Middle East conflict is resolved. Diesel averaged 199.5p a litre in October, the highest since records began 30 years ago, the ONS said.

Chief secretary to the Treasury Emma Reynolds pointed to government help: VAT on electricity has been cut to zero until next July, and 20 million homes are due a £100 payment in January. Cornwall Insight's Craig Lowrey said that support "can only chip away at the edges of a bill that continues to be pushed up by events in the Middle East". Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said affordability was her "number one priority". Shadow chancellor Andrew Griffith blamed "soaring energy costs" and Labour tax and employment decisions. The Daily Mail grouped the bill rise with higher mortgage costs as a £1,200 winter "bombshell".

## 5. British investigators say the crew of Mike Lynch's yacht Bayesian did not know about a critical wind risk before the vessel sank off Sicily, killing seven people

The crew of the superyacht Bayesian did not know about a critical safety risk that left the vessel vulnerable in powerful winds before it sank off northern Sicily, Britain's Marine Accident Investigation Branch said in its final report, published on Thursday. British tech tycoon Mike Lynch, 59, and his 18-year-old daughter Hannah were among seven people who died when the 56-metre yacht sank on 19 August 2024. Fifteen people survived.

The MAIB said a downburst of hurricane-force winds knocked the yacht onto its side in less than 15 seconds, and that the speed of events overtook the crew. Wind speeds went from 8 knots to about 70 knots within minutes, far above a forecast of gusts up to 27 knots. Investigators said the yacht had "a vulnerability to high winds that was not recognised, because the yacht had never been assessed at anchor with its sails down". A study for the MAIB found that once the vessel passed an angle of about 73 degrees it could not right itself. The centreboard was raised, as was usual at anchor, and none of the risks was covered in the UK training syllabus or in published guidance available to the crew, the report said. It also found that alcohol consumption had not contributed to the sinking.

The British findings appeared to contrast with recent reports from Italy, where prosecution experts have reportedly blamed errors by the crew. Captain James Cutfield, chief engineer Tim Parker Eaton and deckhand Matthew Griffith are under investigation in Italy over potential charges of multiple manslaughter and culpable shipwreck. The MAIB said Italian prosecutors had restricted access to some witnesses and evidence, and that the picture might change if that material is disclosed. It made seven recommendations, including new rules for single-hulled sailing vessels and better sharing of severe weather forecasts.

## 6. Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin hold a first three-way meeting in Shenzhen, after one-on-one talks in which Trump calls Putin 'my friend'

The leaders of the United States, China and Russia are holding a first three-way meeting in Shenzhen on Thursday, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. Trump called Putin "my friend" and spoke of an "excellent relationship". The BBC reported that Xi personally met Trump at the airport, a first for a foreign leader.

The agenda is expected to include the wars in Ukraine and Iran, and nuclear arms control. The last major US-Russian treaty, New START, expired in February. Trump has said he wants China included in any new limits, a step Beijing has resisted. For Xi, hosting the talks is a display of China's central place in global politics, though the BBC said he will not be keen to put Ukraine at the centre of the discussion and must manage two unpredictable counterparts. The BBC's Russia editor, Steve Rosenberg, said the gathering is a diplomatic coup for Putin, who can show that efforts to isolate Russia have failed, even as the war in Ukraine continues.

European governments and Ukraine have watched the talks with concern, fearing they could be left outside any bargain. Volodymyr Zelensky has said Trump and Xi could press Putin to end the war if they wanted to. Reuters reported that Trump called the summit a "win" before the three-way session began. The Mail and CNN noted that a military officer was seen carrying Russia's nuclear briefcase, or "football", on the trip.

## 15. European leaders gather in Berlin for a Coalition of the Willing meeting on Ukraine, with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner expected to attend

Heads of government from around 20 countries, mostly in Europe, were due in Berlin on Thursday for a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing on Ukraine's future. The group, which brings together 36 countries and is led by Germany, France and the United Kingdom, was formed to keep military and financial support flowing and, in principle, to help monitor a ceasefire and offer security guarantees if one is agreed. It first met in London in March 2025.

DW said extensive security measures were in place in Berlin's government district. It was not clear whether President Volodymyr Zelensky would attend. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were expected, according to several reports, including TASS, which said they would discuss the next multilateral talks with Ukrainian and European delegates. The meeting comes on the same day as Donald Trump holds talks with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, a contrast Al Jazeera drew in a piece on Europe counting the cost of Ukraine's defence far from that table.

Stefan Meister of the German Council on Foreign Relations told DW that Europe had still not put forward a clear plan for the security guarantees or troops it would offer. UK outlets did not give the Berlin meeting prominent play in the homepages and feeds we checked, despite Britain being one of the group's leaders.
