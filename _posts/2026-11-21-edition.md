## 1. Andy Burnham signs a UK-EU deal on food trade, emissions and youth mobility in Brussels and says Britain will explore even closer ties, as a row over student fees is left unresolved

The prime minister, Andy Burnham, agreed a package of deals with the European Union at a summit in Brussels on Friday, covering British food exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme. Most checks on British food exports will be scrapped in return for the UK following some EU regulations from 2028. The youth scheme will allow up to 100,000 people aged 18 to 31 from each side to travel, work and study for up to three years, with places due to be available no later than January 2030.

Burnham called it a "good day" for Britain and Europe and said the Treasury believed the deal could add up to £17bn a year to the economy. He said the UK would now discuss options for even closer ties. European Council President António Costa said the EU was "open to have the closest possible relationship". Legislation is expected in Parliament next week.

A proposal from Brussels to cut tuition fees for EU students at British universities was not agreed, and a further six months has been set aside to discuss it. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the BBC the deal would come into force regardless. Kemi Badenoch said the deal "isn't fair" and Nigel Farage called it a "betrayal of the British people".

Saturday's front pages split sharply. The Daily Mail led with "Ambushed", the Mirror praised "common-sense deals", the Express reacted with "scorn", the Telegraph reported he was ready to discuss rejoining the EU, and the Times quoted an ally saying he believes "deeply" that the UK should return.

## 3. Anti-migrant activist Daniel Thomas, known online as Danny Tommo, is jailed for 15 months for slashing a dinghy in the Channel while a French rescue worker was on board

Daniel Thomas, a 37-year-old anti-migrant activist who calls himself Danny Tommo, has been jailed for 15 months for slashing an inflatable boat in the Channel that had been carrying asylum seekers. He had pleaded guilty to criminal damage earlier this month and was sentenced at Portsmouth Crown Court on Friday.

The court heard that on 22 September Thomas, founder of the anti-migration group Patriot Platform, took a boat into the Channel and followed a dinghy as asylum seekers were helped by the RNLI, Border Force and the French coastguard. After the migrants had been taken off, he approached the empty dinghy, which a French rescue worker had boarded, and stabbed it with a knife. The judge, James Newton-Price KC, counted about eight cuts. The incident was livestreamed, and Thomas could be heard saying "this is what needs to happen".

The judge accepted there had been "no immediate danger" to the rescue worker but said the behaviour would have been highly alarming to anyone nearby. He called Thomas a "publicity seeker" and said the offence was "a flagrant interference and a deliberate disruption" of the work of the Border Force, coastguard and RNLI volunteers. Because it was livestreamed, he said, there must have been a "high degree of planning or premeditation".

Most UK outlets carried the sentence. Outlets differed mainly on the label: the Guardian and Independent called him far-right, the BBC, Mirror and Express anti-migrant, and the Sun hard-right, reporting that he sobbed in the dock. Al Jazeera, DW and AP also covered it.

## 6. Poland says a Russian cruise missile crashed on its soil during one of Moscow's biggest air attacks on Ukraine, calling it an 'intentional provocation' as Zelensky calls Putin a 'killer'

Poland says a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile was among three objects that violated its airspace early on Friday, landing in a rural area at Nowy Lipowiec in the south-east of the country, about 50km from the Ukrainian border. Nobody was hurt and no damage was reported. Another suspected missile came down near Rudnik nad Sanem and a jet-powered drone crashed near Mołodycz, according to Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz, who said he would ask Nato for additional support.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the incident did not look like an accident, coming as Western allies held military exercises in the region. "It appears that we are dealing with an intentional provocation," he said. Polish F-16s and Swedish Gripens were among the Nato and allied aircraft that responded. Nato Secretary General Mark Rutte condemned what he called "grave violations". Russia did not comment, the BBC reported.

The incursions came during what President Volodymyr Zelensky called the biggest Russian attack of the year, with 110 missiles and more than 400 drones targeting bridges in Kyiv and railway stations. The BBC said at least nine people were killed, while CBS reported at least eight. Zelensky told CNN that whatever Donald Trump called Putin, "for us, he's a killer". At the White House, Trump said Putin would "love to end the war", and the Daily Mail reported that he called the strike on Poland a "mistake".

British outlets stressed how close the missiles came to allied troops on exercise: the Times said "miles from British troops", and the Mirror said its reporters were at an exercise about 30 miles away.

## 15. Three Paris police officers are convicted of a racist assault on Black music producer Michel Zecler, whose 2020 beating was caught on camera, and banned from policing for life

Three French police officers have been convicted of beating Black music producer Michel Zecler in Paris in 2020, with the court upholding racism as an aggravating circumstance. The officers had stopped Zecler outside his music studio on 21 November 2020, accusing him of not wearing a mandatory face mask, and beat him in an assault recorded by his studio's security cameras. The footage showed he suffered about 30 punches, kicks and baton strikes while offering little resistance, the BBC reported.

The officers were also found guilty of signing a falsified police report claiming Zecler had violently resisted arrest. Aurélien Lapersonne, 29, Philippe Tondeux, 50, and Pierre Plantier, 37, each received five-year sentences with different portions suspended, and Plantier will serve his one year with an ankle monitor. All three were banned from ever serving in the police again. The officers admitted the beating in court but denied using a racial slur.

The Guardian said the trial was considered a turning point for the recognition of racist violence in France. The case became a cause célèbre for critics of the police, and the BBC noted the verdict came as police again face criticism over their handling of recent protests. France 24 called it a rare ruling, and the New York Times and Al Jazeera also covered it.
