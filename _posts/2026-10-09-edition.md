## 1. ADHD and autism at risk of over-diagnosis, government review warns

A government-commissioned review of mental health, ADHD and autism in England, led by Prof Peter Fonagy, has found an increasing risk that people are being over-diagnosed with ADHD and autism, warning that the country may be at a "tipping point" where diagnoses outstrip the true number of people with the conditions. The 607-page report, contributed to by 120 experts, estimated that 3.8% of 5 to 15-year-olds have ADHD, while NHS data shows 3.88% of 12 to 17-year-olds now have a diagnosis after a 15-fold rise since 2000. It called for tougher regulation of private assessment firms and a ban on their advertising, and for access to care to be based on need so that people do not wait years for a formal diagnosis. "Diagnosis remains essential and must be protected, what has to change is that it is no longer the only door through which help can be reached," Fonagy said.

The review also found that mental distress has risen substantially across all age groups since the 1990s, especially among the young: the share of 16 to 64-year-olds with a common mental disorder rose from 15.5% in 1993 to 22.6% in 2024, and from about one in six to one in four among 16 to 24-year-olds. It said economic uncertainty and social media were driving the increase. The review was ordered by Wes Streeting when he was health secretary, after he claimed young people were over-diagnosed; Health Secretary Yvette Cooper said it showed "the importance of early intervention." Several of Friday's front pages lead on the report, with emphasis ranging from harm caused by labels to the reality of young people's distress.

## 2. Search warrants used in Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor raids ruled unlawful

Thames Valley Police has accepted that the search warrants it used to raid Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor's homes in February were unlawful, the High Court heard on Thursday. Mr Justice Hilliard said the force had obtained the warrants under the wrong statutory provision and quashed them, but added: "The fact that the search warrants were quashed does not bring the investigation to an end or mean that the whole of the investigation is unlawful." The warrants were issued by an Old Bailey judge on 18 February, the day before the former prince was arrested on his 66th birthday on suspicion of misconduct in public office, over allegations that he shared sensitive information with Jeffrey Epstein while UK trade envoy. He denies wrongdoing.

Police searched his residence on the Sandringham estate and Royal Lodge in Windsor and seized documents and electronic devices, which they will keep for now; a further hearing, expected in 2027, will decide whether the material can be retained and used, which Mountbatten-Windsor opposes. AP reported that police had conceded in July that the warrants were improper. The Independent, Sun and Mirror lead on the ruling, while the Telegraph quotes friends saying his "fightback starts now."

## 3. Labour holds Holborn and St Pancras as Polanski falls short

Labour's Sagal Abdi-Wali, the leader of Camden council, won the Holborn and St Pancras by-election overnight, defeating Green Party leader Zack Polanski in the seat vacated by former prime minister Sir Keir Starmer. She took 12,292 votes (45%) to Polanski's 8,928 (33%), a majority of 3,364, with the Conservatives a distant third on 1,922 and Reform UK fourth. Turnout was 38.7%, which the Guardian says is the lowest for any by-election in England this parliament, and the Standard reports a 13% swing to the Greens. ## 4. Latvians arrested inside RAF Molesworth as Navy sailor is charged over Russia spying

The men were initially arrested on suspicion of trespass on a prohibited site and criminal damage, then further arrested under section 4 of the National Security Act 2023 on suspicion of entering a prohibited place for a purpose prejudicial to the UK.
