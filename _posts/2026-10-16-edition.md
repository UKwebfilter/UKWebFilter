## 1. Healey weighs a Budget tax raid on bank profits

Senior banking executives told the BBC they expect the sector to be "on the menu" when Chancellor John Healey delivers his Budget in two weeks. Healey has called the banks part of "the crown jewels" of the UK economy but has refused to rule out higher taxes on them. The Telegraph led its front page with "Healey plans tax raid on banks", and the Guardian reported that one option under discussion would reverse the cut to the bank surcharge made under Jeremy Hunt, raising upwards of £2bn a year.

Banks currently pay a 3% surcharge on top of the 25% corporation tax rate, plus a levy on their balance sheets. The levy raised £1.4bn last year and the surcharge £1.2bn, and the BBC said the sector contributed nearly £40bn to the Treasury including employees' income tax. The four biggest banks made around £15bn in profit in the first half of the year, prompting calls from unions for more tax. Another option discussed with officials is a temporary limit on using past losses to offset profits, worth an estimated £1bn a year. Trade body UK Finance warned higher taxes would weaken competitiveness and investment, and banks told the Telegraph a bigger surcharge would cost the economy £20bn. The Treasury said decisions on tax are for the chancellor.

## 4. Bus hits train at Channel Tunnel terminal in France, injuring 19

A minibus carrying lorry drivers hit a departing freight shuttle train at a loading platform at the Channel Tunnel terminal in Coquelles, near Calais, injuring the driver and 18 passengers, French authorities said. The collision happened at 19:27 local time (18:27 BST) on Thursday. The bus driver and one passenger were seriously injured and taken away by helicopter, but none of the injuries were life threatening, a local official said.

Sub-prefect Agathe Cury said two trains were either side of the platform, one being loaded with heavy goods vehicles and another departing, and "for a reason that currently remains unknown" the minibus collided with the departing train. The bus did not overturn. All those on board were men of different nationalities, and it was not clear whether any were British. Cury said it was the first incident of its kind on a Eurotunnel loading platform, and an investigation has begun. Freight and Eurostar traffic was not disrupted beyond a brief stoppage to evacuate the injured, she said, and Eurostar said its London services would run as expected. The BBC's Evan Davis, travelling through at the time, saw emergency vehicles on one platform while cars still moved through check-in. AP and the Washington Post reported that service was halted.

## 5. NHS England tells hospitals to place trans patients by biological sex

NHS England has issued guidance saying trans patients should be cared for on single-sex wards or bays that match their biological sex rather than their gender identity. Where that is not "clinically appropriate", alternative arrangements should be made while maintaining the dignity and safety of all patients. Staff changing rooms and toilets should also be used according to biological sex, and trusts are asked to audit whether they offer enough alternatives such as gender neutral or single-occupancy toilets.

Earlier guidance had allowed patients and staff to use wards and facilities according to gender identity. It was withdrawn after the Supreme Court ruled in April 2025 that "sex" in equality law means biological sex. The new guidance says trans people should be offered gender neutral spaces to avoid discrimination, that some decisions will be made case by case, and that clinical need comes first in emergency and urgent care. NHS England data shows 5,520 breaches of mixed-sex accommodation rules in August 2026, up from 3,853 in June 2025. Sex Matters called the guidance a "momentous shift back to reality" but raised concerns about patient records. TransActual and Trans+ Solidarity Alliance said staff would be unclear how to care for trans patients safely. The Mail said the change came 18 months after the court ruling.

## 6. Teenager kills three children and a teacher at Oregon primary school

A 16-year-old shot dead three children and a teacher at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon, before being killed by a bystander, police said. Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz said the suspect forced his way into the school on Thursday afternoon by shooting at locked doors. The children killed were aged five, eight and 11. A seven-year-old and a 54-year-old teacher were taken to hospital in a critical condition.

Police said the first call reported someone shooting at a car with a long gun, and minutes later 911 calls said the attacker had entered the school. A community member who saw the shooting begin pursued him inside and shot him dead; officers arrived three minutes later. Police described the suspect as a local boy and did not name him or the victims, and it was unclear whether he had any link to the school. Around 450 children attend Silver Rail, which has more than 40 staff. Schools in Bend were closed on Friday. The district's top official called it a "senseless tragedy", and Oregon Governor Kotek offered support to the community, NBC reported. The BBC cited the K-12 School Shooting Database as recording 235 shooting incidents at US schools last year and at least 140 so far this year. CGTN, Al Jazeera, Times of Israel and Arab News also carried the story.
