## 1. Andy Burnham says he wants an 'honest discussion' about a return to free movement with the EU, drawing accusations of Brexit betrayal

Prime Minister Andy Burnham says he would welcome an "honest discussion" about the prospect of returning to free movement between the UK and the European Union, the BBC reported. Speaking to the Sunday Times, he said he wants to pursue a new type of relationship with Brussels, which could include full EU membership or joining the single market, which would require free movement. "I think you have to have a very factual, quite evidence-based debate about this," he said.

Burnham acknowledged that free movement "had very significant challenges associated with it", but suggested migration had changed since the 2016 referendum. "Revolving door migration where people would come and then go back is different," he said. He added that he could not see himself recommending joining the euro or the Schengen area. He has previously committed to Labour's 2024 manifesto, which ruled out a return to free movement, the single market and a customs union, and has said fundamental change would need a mandate at a general election.

Shadow home secretary Chris Philp said there could be "no return" to free movement, and Reform UK leader Nigel Farage called the comments a "total betrayal", the Guardian reported. Burnham is due to meet EU leaders at a summit in Brussels on 20 November.

## 3. Storm Boelo brings amber warnings and gusts of up to 80mph to Scotland, Northern Ireland and northern England

Amber weather warnings have come into force in Scotland, Northern Ireland and northern England as Storm Boelo moves across the UK, with gusts of up to 80mph forecast in the worst-hit areas, the BBC reported. The amber warning for Scotland and England stays in place until 21:00 on Sunday, while Northern Ireland's runs until 10:00. A wider yellow wind warning covers much of Scotland, northern England, parts of the Midlands and north Wales.

By Saturday evening gusts of 75mph had been recorded at South Uist in the Western Isles, according to the Met Office. Train and ferry services were cancelled in Scotland, police warned against unnecessary journeys and flooding closed several roads. More than 2,000 properties in north-west England were without power on Saturday night, while National Grid was still restoring supplies in south-west England after Storm Austen.

The Met Office warned of possible power cuts, flying debris and danger to life from large waves, and advised people to "stay indoors as much as possible". The BBC said an oil tanker 350 nautical miles off Mull issued a distress alert because of large swells and an RAF aircraft was sent, but no assistance was needed. The Daily Mirror said 28 areas were on flood alert.

## 4. Budget to include more than £1bn for social and affordable housing and new money and borrowing powers for England's mayors

More than £1bn for social and affordable housing in England will be provided in Wednesday's Budget, taking funding for the government's 10-year homes programme past £40bn, the BBC reported. The Daily Mirror, which first reported the figure, said the money could help build up to 10,000 new council homes.

Mayors and regional leaders are also set to get greater powers and funding. An initial £285m of transitional "good growth" funding will be allocated next year, and the Treasury will work with mayors on a new £1.5bn borrowing facility from 2028 that could be spent on homes, high streets or transport. The Guardian said Chancellor John Healey would also announce £150m to help local authorities build expertise, amid Treasury concern about whether some can manage larger budgets, and that some income tax revenue would be handed directly to areas from 2028.

"I'm calling time on local leaders going cap in hand to Westminster. That is no way to run a country," Prime Minister Andy Burnham said. Healey said the plans meant "more money, more power and more capacity" for local areas. Claire Ward, chair of the UK Mayors Network, welcomed the announcement. The BBC said the Budget would also include support for small breweries.

## 6. Almost 1,000 arrested in France as clashes follow a largely peaceful day of mass protests over living costs and schools

Nearly 1,000 people were arrested as hundreds of thousands took part in another day of protests across France, followed by clashes late on Saturday, the BBC reported. The interior ministry estimated 410,000 people marched, including 110,000 in Paris, while unions put the figure at about 800,000. Some 70,000 police officers were deployed and restrictions imposed in several cities after last weekend's rioting and looting.

Clashes broke out in Paris at the end of a peaceful march from Porte d'Italie, with police using tear gas and water cannon. Barricades were set alight in Marseille, protesters pulled boards from a shop in Bordeaux to build barricades, and police used tear gas in Lyon. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said several thousand radical activists gathered at the rear of the Paris march, including about 500 identified as rioters, and that two people were seriously injured.

CBS News said Nuñez described an "extremely elevated level of violence" from "ultra-left" groups later in the day, but said looting was on a far smaller scale than a week earlier, when some 3,000 businesses were damaged or looted. The New York Times said the large police presence, the arrests and rerouted marches kept a lid on wholesale vandalism. The Times of Israel said the protests were against low school funding and soaring living costs.

## 7. Brazilians vote in a knife-edge presidential run-off between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro

Brazilians go to the polls on Sunday in a closely fought presidential run-off between the 80-year-old left-wing incumbent, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who is seeking a fourth non-consecutive term, and right-wing senator Flávio Bolsonaro, 45, the BBC reported. Bolsonaro is the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27-year sentence for plotting a coup after losing the 2022 election and has pledged to release him if elected.

Flávio Bolsonaro won the first round on 4 October by 47% to 45%, and the two are now tied in the polls, the Guardian said. No candidate who trailed after the first round has gone on to win the run-off since Brazil's return to democracy. Nearly 159 million people are eligible to vote.

On Saturday more than a million Lula supporters packed Rio de Janeiro's Copacabana beach, according to the Guardian, where Lula told the crowd the election was a question of "sovereignty". His government has accused the US of interfering in the election, an allegation Washington has called "baseless". Bolsonaro, who has promised a crackdown on organised crime and cuts to taxes and spending, held his final rally in Belo Horizonte. Al Jazeera said both candidates drew thousands to election-eve rallies.
