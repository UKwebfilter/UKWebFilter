## 1. Thomas Markle, father of Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, is charged in Florida over alleged possession of child sex abuse material and placed under electronic monitoring

Thomas Markle, the 82-year-old father of Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, has appeared in a Florida federal court after being charged over alleged possession of child sex abuse material, the BBC and Guardian reported. US prosecutors say he was arrested at a senior care facility in Polk County after the FBI searched two Los Angeles-area storage units he had rented for nearly two decades. Agents say they seized about 78 items, including film negatives, slides, photographs and books that they allege contained illegal material.

A federal complaint alleges some images showed Markle with prepubescent girls. Witnesses were said to have identified him in two of the images; the man pictured appeared to be in his 30s or 40s, suggesting the alleged material may date back decades. Markle has previously denied allegations of possessing such images, calling them "sick lies". The Duke and Duchess of Sussex have not commented.

FBI director Kash Patel said authorities "moved quickly" and that Markle "allegedly targeted innocent children". Bill Essayli, first assistant US attorney for the central district of California, said the allegations were extremely serious and would be prosecuted to the fullest extent of federal law. ## 3. A third wave of hoax bomb threats hits European flights and media outlets, with Hungarian jets scrambled and no explosives found

European airlines faced a third wave of bomb threats in a week on Friday, the BBC and Guardian reported. Alerts affected at least 18 flights to or from Bulgaria, Cyprus, Czechia, France, Hungary, Italy, Portugal, Romania and the UK. Hungarian fighter jets escorted civilian flights three times, defence minister Romulusz Ruszin-Szendi said. Searches found no explosives and aircraft landed safely. Earlier waves this week had already forced dozens of diversions and tarmac searches.

Irish public broadcaster RTÉ and other Dublin newsrooms including the Irish Independent, Newstalk and TodayFM were evacuated after similar warnings. Sky said it also received a security threat. Irish police said they were aware of threats against media organisations in Ireland and Europe. The Irish government called it a "likely coordinated campaign of disruption". Taoiseach Micheál Martin said such threats sow fear and must be taken seriously. No concrete link has been established between the airline and media messages.

A Russian ultra-right milieu styled "Fighters of the Male State" had been linked in earlier reporting to signed emails; the group has denied sending the hoaxes. Italian prime minister Giorgia Meloni said it was unclear whether the flight hoaxes and recent supermarket cyber-attacks were connected. Eurocontrol figures cited in coverage put Friday's affected flights at a tiny share of scheduled European traffic.

## 4. UK house prices fall at the steepest annual rate in more than three years in October as higher mortgage costs deter buyers

Average UK house prices fell 1.2% in the year to October 2026, the steepest annual decline since October 2023, Lloyds Banking Group said, the Daily Mail reported. It was the fourth consecutive monthly fall. The typical home was valued at about £296,285 after a 0.6% month-on-month drop, or about £1,882, versus September. The reading was weaker than analysts' forecast of a small monthly rise. Greater London and the south-east and south-west of England saw some of the biggest falls.

Lloyds said higher mortgage rates had prompted more prospective buyers to delay moves, with surging energy bills and grocery costs adding pressure. A glut of homes for sale, notably flats, has forced more competitive pricing. Andrew Asaam, mortgages director at Lloyds, said renewed inflationary pressures, driven in part by the Middle East conflict, were keeping borrowing costs high and making buyers more cautious.

The Bank of England raised its base rate by 0.25 points to 4% on Thursday, the first increase in more than three years. Rightmove said the average five-year fixed mortgage was 5.5% at the end of October, up from 5% at the end of June. Separately, the BBC led a household explainer on rising rates under the line "My mortgage is going up £200", describing how mortgages, credit cards and loans could get more expensive for millions.
