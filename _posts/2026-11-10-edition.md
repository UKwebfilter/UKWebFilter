## 3. US military aircraft are moved out of RAF Mildenhall in Suffolk after a reported threat from Iran, as security is raised at two British bases

The US has moved military aircraft out of RAF Mildenhall in Suffolk after a threat from Iran, according to a Wall Street Journal report followed by the Daily Mirror and Daily Express. Two US refuelling tankers using the base diverted to Germany on Monday and several others were moved to Germany and other locations later in the day, according to flight-tracking data and an official cited in the reports. It is not yet clear whether the threat is imminent or more general, and US officials are still discussing its nature.

The US raised the security posture at RAF Mildenhall and RAF Lakenheath to FPCON Charlie, the third-highest level, which applies when intelligence points to likely terrorist action. A US Air Force spokesperson declined to give details but said aircraft rotate between locations for many reasons, and the White House referred questions to the Pentagon. The move follows last month's withdrawal of all 12 B-1 bombers from RAF Fairford, which was reportedly driven by concern about an Iran-backed plot that Iran denied. Asked about the concern, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said: "We've addressed it by moving people away from the threat." It came hours after Donald Trump said the Iran war was "effectively over" militarily.

## 4. Pupils in England will no longer be allowed to keep phones in their bags or pockets under stricter school guidance from September 2027

Pupils in England will not be allowed to have their phones on them at any point in the school day under strengthened government guidance, the BBC, Daily Mail and Daily Mirror reported. Statutory guidance in force since June already makes schools responsible for being phone-free, but many schools have allowed "not seen, not heard" policies in which phones stay in bags. Ministers say the updated guidance, coming into force from September 2027, will go further and require that phones are inaccessible throughout the day. Department for Education polling found only 16% of secondary pupils said phone rules were followed all of the time.

Schools can choose how to comply, using sealed pouches, lockers, collecting phones on arrival or banning them from sites. Education Secretary Lucy Powell said "school is for learning and friendship, not scrolling". No new funding is expected, the Mirror said, and head teachers' unions warned of the cost: Pepe Di'Iasio of the Association of School and College Leaders said the plan was "not feasible" without funding, while NAHT's Paul Whiteman said stronger measures "come at a cost". Children's Commissioner Dame Rachel de Souza welcomed the change. Teacher Tapp data found 39% of secondary schools now collect phones, up from 19% in 2025.

Several boroughs, including Westminster, Wandsworth and Tower Hamlets, were covered by the highest pollution readings, the Mirror said. It is the capital's fourth high pollution alert this year, the last coming in a June heatwave. Deputy mayor Mete Coban said toxic air is responsible for thousands of premature deaths in London each year. ## 7. Russia, Ukraine and the US hold their first three-way peace talks in more than nine months in Abu Dhabi, as Washington seeks a "final end" to the war

Negotiators from Russia, Ukraine and the United States are meeting in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday for their first trilateral talks in more than nine months, France 24 and the Guardian reported. US officials said they would seek a "final end" to the war, and the Guardian said part of the Trump administration's proposal involves a new "long-term security architecture" between Russia and Europe. A US official said the negotiators could revive the Nato-Russia council suspended after the 2022 invasion and potentially lift sanctions on Russia to reach a deal, and said Ukraine could be offered security guarantees "very similar to Nato", which would be for Donald Trump and Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss.

European officials are concerned that Russia may hold to its demand that Nato roll back its military footprint in eastern Europe, the Guardian said. France 24 reported that Kyiv faces pressure to make territorial concessions, and the Telegraph quoted US negotiators saying Ukraine was showing "flexibility" on territory. CGTN reported that European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU would take part, while the Kremlin insisted the talks were for Russia, Ukraine and the US only.
