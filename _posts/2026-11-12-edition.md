## 1. Ministers warn the TV and entertainment industry to protect women and girls or face a crackdown, after the David Walliams allegations, which he denies, and Russell Brand's jailing

Ministers have told the entertainment industry to bring in stronger protections for women and children or face government action, after allegations against David Walliams and the jailing of Russell Brand. Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy will meet leaders from TV, film, publishing and possibly sport next week. BBC Newsnight reported that she will tell them to take responsibility for safeguarding themselves, but that if their response is not good enough the government could step in, including by making membership of the Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority (CIISA), a whistleblowing body that is currently voluntary, compulsory.

Walliams, 55, denies all the allegations made in a joint Guardian and ITV News investigation, saying he is "not a sexual predator" and has "not broken the law". Brand was jailed for more than 10 years on Monday for rape and sexual assault. Downing Street called the Walliams allegations "deeply troubling" and spoke of "a pattern of behaviour that needs to change". Women and Equalities minister Bridget Phillipson said there was "scope to go further" in curbing non-disclosure agreements, and Justice Secretary Alex Norris told peers there was "a lot of concern" about powerful men using the legal system to silence accusers. Jonathan Ross said he had been unaware of either man's conduct, despite a 2004 awards joke about a 15-year-old "going home" with Walliams, which he said was written by the show's scriptwriters.

## 2. A government-commissioned review by Alan Milburn will say some under-25s should get less in benefits, moving young people off the main sickness and disability payments

Young people under 25 could receive less in benefits under a radical overhaul to be recommended by a government-commissioned review led by the former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn. His year-long report, due on Tuesday, is expected to recommend that under-25s can no longer claim the health element of universal credit or personal independence payment (Pip). Instead they would receive an allowance at the basic universal credit rate, with a new "additional cost allowance" for some, and a higher-rate benefit for those with severe disabilities who cannot work. The Daily Express reported that young people with conditions such as ADHD and anxiety could lose access to the health element, worth around £217 a month to new claimants.

Milburn told the Times the plans would not be "pain-free" and that "some people will get less financial support at the beginning of their benefit claim". He is also expected to call for billions to be invested in work placements, job subsidies and personal advisers, with young people at risk of losing some benefits if they do not take part. Just under one million young people are not in education, employment or training. The i Paper reported that Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden are expected to accept the recommendations, though some Labour MPs have threatened to rebel. The BBC said savings could take almost a decade to appear.

## 4. Andy Burnham heads to the Cop31 climate summit promising British climate leadership, after a £12bn offshore wind deal and a backlash over North Sea oil and gas

Andy Burnham is due to speak at the Cop31 climate summit in Turkey on Thursday, where the Independent reported he will promise a "new era of British climate leadership", as his government tries to repair green credentials damaged by its approval of the Jackdaw gasfield in the North Sea. The BBC said he had been pressed on the Rosebank oil field ahead of the speech.

Before the summit the government announced a £12bn investment by the German energy company RWE and the US private equity firm KKR in the Vanguard West and Vanguard East offshore windfarms off the Norfolk coast, which it said was believed to be the biggest of its kind in UK history. The projects will also receive £1bn from the state-owned National Wealth Fund, are expected to generate about 2.8 gigawatts, enough to supply three million homes, and should be producing power by 2030, with construction starting early next year. RWE estimated they would support 2,300 jobs a year during construction. Burnham called the deal a "vote of confidence for building in Britain" and said every turbine was "power under British control". Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said Britain was "leading from the front". The Guardian reported that Burnham has faced a backlash from Labour MPs and environmental campaigners over Jackdaw, and that ministers on Tuesday rejected the £800m Botley West solar farm near Blenheim Palace because of harm to the green belt.

## 5. Police name Brenda Raymond, 80, who was deliberately set on fire outside her home in Watford, as detectives investigating her attempted murder appeal for help

Detectives investigating the attempted murder of an 80-year-old woman who was set on fire outside her home in Watford have named her as Brenda Raymond, and her family have released her photograph to help the appeal for information. Police were called at 12.47pm on Monday 9 November to reports that a woman had been found on fire in a first-floor communal area of a block of flats in Caractacus Cottage View. She suffered life-threatening injuries and remains in a critical condition in hospital.

Detectives believe she was attacked as she returned home and that an accelerant may have been used before she was deliberately set alight. Evidence recovered at the scene also suggested that a flammable liquid may have been poured through the letterbox of another property nearby. Detective Inspector Adam Bridges, of the Major Crime Unit, called it "an appalling and deeply distressing attack on an elderly woman" and a "highly unusual incident", and said her family were "understandably distraught". He urged anyone with information, however small, to come forward. Police want to hear from anyone who saw suspicious behaviour in the area between 10am and 2pm that day, saw someone wearing a face covering or running towards nearby woodland or footpaths, heard screams or a disturbance, has CCTV, doorbell or dashcam footage, or knows of anyone who bought flammable liquid around that date. The investigation has been named Operation Tibbin.

## 7. Democrat Scott Colom wins the Senate race in Mississippi, becoming the state's first Black US senator since Reconstruction in a stunning upset
