## 1. Covid inquiry finds government failures let the virus spread like wildfire in care homes

Government failures created an environment that allowed Covid to spread "like wildfire" in care homes, the UK Covid inquiry has found. In its sixth report, published on Tuesday, inquiry chair Baroness Heather Hallett said the adult social care sector appeared to be "something of an afterthought" in the early stages of the pandemic, and that former health secretary Matt Hancock's claim to have thrown a "protective ring" around care homes was "unfounded". More than 43,000 deaths involving Covid were recorded in UK care homes between March 2020 and July 2022.

The 287-page report found that a lack of protective equipment left staff using bin bags, festival ponchos and painters' overalls, the BBC reported. It said the decision to discharge hospital patients into care homes was understandable, but that the failure to advise that they be isolated was a significant oversight. The Guardian noted Hancock had taken responsibility for discharging about 25,000 patients before mandatory testing began, a policy later ruled unlawful by the High Court. The report also found evidence of blanket do-not-resuscitate orders in some homes and said limits on visits caused immense emotional harm.

Jean Adamson of Covid-19 Bereaved Families for Justice UK called Hancock's claim "a cruel lie", while saying the recommendations did not go far enough. Channel 4 News reported that Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the government was committed to learning the lessons.

## 3. Government set to approve the Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea despite objections from climate campaigners

The UK government is set to approve drilling at the Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea on Wednesday, the first big new field in almost a decade, according to the BBC, The Guardian and The Times. The Guardian reported that Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh and Chancellor John Healey would make the announcement in Aberdeen, and that Fahnbulleh had decided not to block the field, allowing the North Sea Transition Authority to give the go-ahead. The BBC said a decision on the Rosebank oil field off Shetland was not expected yet.

Jackdaw, about 150 miles east of Aberdeen, is operated by Shell and Equinor and would account for 6% of North Sea gas production when fully operational. Its operators say it could be connected to the UK network within months, and The Times said North Sea gas production would increase within weeks. Labour's 2024 manifesto pledged no new licences but to honour existing ones. The field was approved by the Conservative government in 2022, but a court ruled last year that its environmental approval had failed to consider the emissions from burning the gas.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has promised a "pragmatic approach" to oil and gas. Tessa Khan of the campaign group Uplift told The Guardian that Jackdaw would provide "just 2% of our gas demand for roughly 10 years", and said Labour's climate credibility was on the line. Industry bodies argue the field will provide jobs and improve energy security.

## 4. RAF Typhoon jets to defend Saudi Arabia from Houthi attacks in the UK's biggest mission under Burnham

Britain will deploy RAF Typhoon fighter jets to Saudi Arabia to help defend the kingdom against escalating attacks by Yemen's Houthi rebels, the government announced on Tuesday. The Ministry of Defence said the aircraft would protect Saudi cities and critical infrastructure from drones and missiles, and stressed that they "will operate in a defensive role and will not conduct offensive strikes against the Houthis". The number of jets was not stated. The Guardian called it the most significant military mission signed off by Andy Burnham as prime minister.

DW reported that the deployment followed a request from Saudi Arabia and that the UK would also send an Atlas A400M transport aircraft and military advisers, and extend air-to-air refuelling for Saudi aircraft for another 90 days. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting called the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia "absolutely despicable", condemning strikes on Riyadh's international airport, which DW said had been targeted 20 times since the start of October.

The Houthi offensive began in September and has escalated into a regional conflict. The Guardian said 19 people have been killed in Saudi Arabia so far, and that a Houthi strike on a Yemeni government training camp on Monday killed 73. HA Hellyer of the Royal United Services Institute warned that the UK could be drawn further into the conflict, asking: "What does defensive mean?" The Times quoted a defence source saying British jets would "take them out" if Houthi drones or missiles crossed the border.

## 5. Two men re-arrested on suspicion of helping Iranian intelligence over the RAF Fairford incident

Two men have been re-arrested on suspicion of assisting a foreign intelligence service in connection with an incident near RAF Fairford last month, counter-terrorism police said on Tuesday. A 24-year-old UK national and a 25-year-old dual UK-Iranian national, both from Westminster, are being held under section 3 of the National Security Act 2023, with police saying the investigation relates to Iran. Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor, head of national counter-terrorism policing, said: "We consider it highly likely that there is Iranian state involvement."

Police were alerted to three suspicious vehicles heading towards the US-used airbase in Gloucestershire shortly after midnight on 27 September. Petrol was found in three vans and five men were arrested, but police later said no improvised explosive devices were found, the BBC reported. The 24-year-old was one of the five arrested near the base, while the 25-year-old was first detained on 1 October. Seven men have been arrested in total, and the other five remain on bail.

The Guardian reported that police believe others involved in the drive to Fairford may not have been aware of what was going on. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said Iran's Revolutionary Guards were almost certainly behind the episode, which led the US to withdraw its B-1 bombers from the base. Iran has denied any involvement. "We know the public have unanswered questions," Taylor said.
