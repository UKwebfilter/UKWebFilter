## 2. Two Iranian men are convicted of spying on London-based journalists and a lawyer for Iran's Revolutionary Guards, with one found to have planned a violent attack

Two Iranian men have been convicted at Woolwich Crown Court of assisting a foreign intelligence service by spying on journalists and a lawyer in the UK on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). Mostafa Sepahvand, 41, and Farhad Javadi Manesh, 46, both from west London, carried out surveillance on two journalists at the London-based Persian broadcaster Iran International, Mojtaba Pourmohsen and Kambiz Hosseini, and on Jonathan Turner, a founding member of UK Lawyers for Israel. A third man, Shapoor Qalehali Khani Noori, 57, was cleared.

Prosecutors said Sepahvand intended to carry out a "violent attack" on at least one target and asked for £150,000 for "finishing off" a target, while Manesh helped with planning. Police seized 534GB of data showing the pair were given pictures of targets, work addresses and car details, and the BBC reported that counter-terrorism police said it was the first time ChatGPT search history had been presented in a state threat case.

The men, asylum seekers with temporary leave to remain, came to the UK illegally, Sepahvand in a lorry in 2016 and Manesh by small boat in 2019. One said he acted under duress, a claim the prosecution called "entirely bogus". The Guardian reported that Hosseini had to leave his home because of threats. DW said sentencing is due in mid-December. Iran's embassy in London rejected espionage allegations against Tehran, IRNA reported.

## 3. The government grants planning permission for an asylum centre for up to 1,256 men at a former military site near Piddington, the Oxfordshire village that symbolically voted to leave the UK

The government has granted planning permission to house up to 1,256 asylum-seeking men at a former Ministry of Defence site near the Oxfordshire village of Piddington. The Home Office's application for the MoD Bicester site was fast-tracked through an Urgent Crown Development application, and a government report said it drew 3,177 representations against and one in support. Housing Secretary Angela Rayner ruled that permission should be granted because "national demand for asylum accommodation remains high".

In September, Piddington voted 285 to 26 to leave the UK in a symbolic referendum organised by opponents of the plan, and campaigners have already taken the proposals to the High Court. The Home Office said the permission lets it continue to progress the site but that "a final decision to proceed is yet to be taken". It said the government was closing all asylum hotels and moving to alternatives, including former military sites, and had saved more than £1bn in costs since the election.

The decision can be challenged in the High Court within six weeks. Shadow home secretary Chris Philp said the government had "betrayed the people of Piddington", and Reform UK's Zia Yusuf said Reform would "detain and deport" the men. Liberal Democrat MP Calum Miller called it "the wrong plan and the wrong site". Parish councillor Joe Marshall said residents were "extremely frustrated". The Daily Mail said the site is one of three former military locations proposed to house a total of 3,750 asylum seekers.

## 5. A BBC investigation finds firms offering to help migrants pose as artists and tech experts to win fast-track 'Global Talent' visas, and the Home Office revokes two visas

Companies are offering to help migrants pose as talented artists and tech experts in order to make fraudulent applications for the UK's Global Talent visa, a BBC investigation has found. The visa was launched in 2020 to attract the "brightest and best" scientists and researchers, and is also open to creative artists. Applicants are not interviewed, can bring their families to the UK, and have a potentially quick path to permanent residency in three or five years.

A consultant from a London company told an undercover BBC reporter that his firm could supply artwork produced in China, as well as arrange exhibitions, media coverage and letters of recommendation. He said no client taking the "fully inclusive" package, costing more than £100,000, had ever been refused. The BBC said he was from iCareer Consultancy UK, based in Canary Wharf.

Following the investigation, the Home Office is revoking visas issued to two Nigerian men who claimed to be fine art photographers, because it believes they were obtained by deception. A Nigerian art critic, Molara Wood, said her name had been attached to online reviews praising their work, and that fake articles were being placed on newspaper websites under the bylines of reputable journalists. A woman who applied through the tech route from India said a company had fabricated evidence for her, including a made-up award; her application was rejected. The Home Office called the findings "extremely serious", said the company was already under investigation and that the route was under review.

## 14. The FBI says North Korean hackers stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency using fake video calls, as US authorities move to seize $1.2 billion from the Binance hack

State-sponsored hackers from North Korea have stolen more than $400 million in cryptocurrency by using fake video calls to steal people's identities, the FBI says. The bureau calls the scheme "FaceStealer" and says more than 900 people have fallen victim in the past 18 months. The US Justice Department said it had seized cloud infrastructure used in the scam, and had court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion taken in the recent hack of the Binance exchange.

According to the Justice Department, the hackers send meeting invitations from hijacked accounts on imposter versions of Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet, record the target's webcam and play clips of a previous victim the target knows. They then stage a fake technical fault and offer a link to fix it, which installs malware. Targets are mostly in crypto and internet fields, increasingly open-source software developers.

"The North Korean regime relies on proceeds from large-scale cryptocurrency theft to fund its illicit weapons program," said FBI cyber director Brett Leatherman. The BBC said Binance revealed a week ago that hackers had used fake job interviews to steal a record $6.3bn; US agencies attributed it to North Korea-linked actors, which Pyongyang denies.
