## 1. Nvidia opens an independent investigation after reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence, as he admits an affair

Nvidia's board said on Sunday it was "initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort", the Guardian, NBC News and CBS News reported. A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will oversee the inquiry. The board said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised" and that it had confidence in Huang, who will remain chief executive.

The reporting, by the New York Times and the Financial Times, centres on Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, no relation, a Chinese businesswoman and former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer. US intelligence officials are reported to have concluded she was an asset of China's Ministry of State Security. In a memo to staff, Huang said: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry." Donald Trump called him "an honorable guy". Republican lawmakers have asked Huang for a closed-door briefing to House committees, and China has dismissed the espionage claims as "groundless speculation", CBS reported.

## 3. The Krakow gunman dies in hospital as Polish prosecutors open a terrorism investigation into the attack that killed six people, including a Briton

The 24-year-old man suspected of killing six people in Krakow's Main Market Square on Saturday has died in hospital, Polish police said early on Monday, the BBC reported. He had been shot by armed officers within three minutes of the attack beginning. Polish prosecutors have opened a terrorism investigation into "the killing of six people … using a firearm, and the attempted murder of many others, including police officers", the Guardian reported, and the interior minister said the suspect held "very radical" and "ultra-nationalistic" views.

Police confirmed the three people found dead at the suspect's home were his father, 53, his mother, 52, and his sister, 21. Four women and two men were killed in the square: three Polish women, a 51-year-old Austrian woman and a 32-year-old British man, a regional official said. Five of the injured were foreign nationals, including a Briton. The two police officers shot while stopping the attacker, praised as "true heroes" by the prime minister, emerged from comas on Sunday, the BBC said. A surgeon who was having lunch nearby told the BBC how he and a colleague rushed to treat the wounded, including the attacker. Poland is holding two days of national mourning.

## 4. The King leads the nation's two-minute silence at the Cenotaph on Remembrance Sunday, joined by a record nine former prime ministers and Andy Burnham for the first time as prime minister

The King led the nation in a two-minute silence and laid the first wreath at the Cenotaph in Whitehall on Remembrance Sunday, dressed in a field marshal's uniform, the Guardian and Mirror reported. The Prince of Wales, Princess Anne and Prince Edward also laid wreaths, while the Princess of Wales watched from a Foreign Office balcony with Queen Camilla, whose wreath was laid by her equerry. About 10,000 veterans marched past the memorial, according to the Mirror, among them 103-year-old Women's Land Army veteran Annie Duplock, taking part for the first time.

The Guardian said a record nine former prime ministers attended as Andy Burnham took part for the first time as prime minister, and that second world war veterans, now about a dozen, outnumbered former prime ministers for what could be the last time. The oldest was RAF veteran Jeffrey Brereton, who served as a mechanic on Hurricanes during the Battle of Britain. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage laid a wreath for the first time after defections took his party to eight MPs. Prince Harry attended a separate service, the Sun and Metro reported. Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy defended linking the UK's return to the Erasmus scheme with Remembrance Sunday after a backlash, the BBC reported.
