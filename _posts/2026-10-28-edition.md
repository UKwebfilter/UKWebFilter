## 1. Healey delivers his first Budget promising households breathing space, with energy bill help, a minimum wage rise and a fuel duty freeze expected

Chancellor John Healey will set out Labour's tax and spending plans when he delivers his first Budget at around 12:30 on Wednesday, the BBC reported. He and Prime Minister Andy Burnham hope to give households "breathing space" from the high cost of living, though Healey has refused to rule out some tax increases. The Budget is expected to include more detail on Burnham's Your First Home scheme, letting first-time buyers in England purchase a new-build home with a 2.5% deposit and a loan worth 20% of the property's value.

A minimum wage increase will be announced, but 18 to 20-year-olds are set to see a smaller rise than in previous years. Typical energy bills are forecast to jump by £276 in January, and Healey has indicated help for households on certain benefits. Tariffs are to be removed from 100 food items, and the BBC understands fuel duty will stay frozen with the 5p cut kept. The Guardian said Healey acknowledged a "difficult moment", would stick to Labour's fiscal rules and is expected to end the carer's allowance cliff edge. The Times reported plans to cut bills by scrapping green levies, while the Daily Mail said the chancellor would duck welfare reform and the Telegraph said 2p could be added to the price of a pint.

Three of Britain's four nuclear deterrent submarines are reportedly at sea at the same time, with at least one understood to be carrying ballistic missiles armed with nuclear warheads, the Daily Mirror reported, citing The Times, which first reported the activity. A Navy source described it as unusual but did not give a reason. The Royal Navy declined to confirm the submarines' tasks, citing security reasons.

Experts believe the other two vessels could be conducting sea trials and preparing for future operations. The fourth Vanguard-class submarine, HMS Victorious, is undergoing deep maintenance at Devonport. Under the Continuous At-Sea Deterrent policy, at least one nuclear-armed submarine is always on patrol, and a former senior naval source said the Navy had long been operating with two boats available at any one time. Patrols have grown longer, with one submarine returning after a record 205 days at sea this year. The Ministry of Defence said the deterrent was "the cornerstone of our national security". ## 7. Defeated Brazilian presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro arrives in the US after Brazil cancels his passports, as Washington grants him and his family visas
