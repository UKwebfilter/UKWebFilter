## 1. Government urges every household to stock up on water and food for emergencies as Streeting warns of state-sponsored terrorism

Every household in Britain is being urged to keep up to nine litres of bottled water per person and three days' worth of food at home under new government guidance designed to help people cope with a weather, cyber or other serious emergency, the Guardian reported. The Be Ready campaign also advises keeping blankets, cash, a first aid kit, a torch with batteries and a wind-up FM radio.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said Britain must prepare for "state-sponsored terrorism" by Russia or other hostile powers targeting everyday infrastructure, saying the threats were "not hypothetical but real". He told BBC One's Question Time the UK faced a greater threat "than we have certainly for most, if not all, of my lifetime", and pointed to the PHP security flaw that took 32 UK government services offline, the BBC reported. Security minister Dan Jarvis called the advice "simple steps to be ready for everyday emergencies".

Streeting said the government wanted people "to be alert but not alarmed", the Mirror reported, though some of the Question Time audience laughed and called it scaremongering. The Daily Mail said ministers had been accused of lecturing families. The guidance led several of Friday's front pages.

## 4. Detective raped by a fellow Met officer says the force's handling of her case was like being raped again and calls on Sir Mark Rowley to resign

A Metropolitan Police detective who was raped by a fellow officer has told the BBC that Scotland Yard's handling of her case was "like being raped all over again". Det Con Linda Williams was attacked in 2004 by PC David Carrick, a serial abuser jailed for life in 2023 for sexual offences against her and 11 other women. She has waived her legal right to anonymity to speak out.

Williams has post-traumatic stress disorder because of the attack and sought damages from the Met for failing to protect her. She told the BBC the lengthy civil case left her feeling suicidal, and that the "worst thing" was when lawyers acting for the commissioner challenged the severity of her PTSD on the basis that she had been raped only once. She has settled the case, and the Met has paid compensation without accepting liability.

In what the BBC called an unprecedented move for a serving officer, she is calling for Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley to resign, saying he has failed to change police culture as promised. The Met said it had tried to reduce further distress to her and to deal with her claims "as quickly and compassionately as possible", and called her continued commitment to public service commendable.

## 7. Magnitude-7 earthquake in Tibet kills at least 16 people and collapses hundreds of homes

A magnitude-7 earthquake has struck China's Tibet region, killing at least 16 people and collapsing hundreds of homes around the northern city of Nagqu, official media said. The quake struck at 10:33pm local time on Thursday, with its epicentre 191km east-northeast of Nagqu in a sparsely populated, high-altitude area, according to the US Geological Survey, which put its depth at 18km, the Guardian reported.

At least 339 homes and 20 livestock sheds collapsed, the Nagqu media office said. Al Jazeera reported 46 people injured, 11 of them seriously, while CBS News and the Guardian put the number of injured at 54, and Reuters later reported that injuries had risen to 65. More than 1,600 people were deployed for rescue efforts, and supplies including 1,000 prefabricated housing units and about 3,000 tents and folding beds arrived in the region.

A level-II emergency response was activated, Xinhua news agency said. The USGS recorded at least seven aftershocks of between 4.3 and 5.2. Videos from state broadcaster CCTV showed workers running out of restaurants and goods falling from supermarket shelves. Al Jazeera said it was the strongest earthquake to hit China since January 2024.
