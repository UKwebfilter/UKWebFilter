## 3. The Bank of England raises interest rates to 4%, the first increase in more than three years, and warns another hike is likely unless the Iran war is resolved

The Bank of England raised its benchmark rate from 3.75% to 4%, the first increase in more than three years, the BBC and Guardian reported. Eight of nine Monetary Policy Committee members backed the quarter-point rise; one preferred a move to 4.25%. Governor Andrew Bailey said the decision should not be seen as the start of an inevitable long sequence of hikes, but that unless the Middle East conflict is resolved it also seemed unlikely to be the last.

The Bank acted against inflation driven mainly by higher fuel prices linked to the Iran war. Consumer prices rose 3.5% in the year to September, and the Bank now expects inflation to peak near 4.9% in mid-2027, or as high as 6.2% in an adverse war scenario. Bailey said there was enough inflationary pressure in the pipeline from energy prices to warrant the rise.

Sky, Channel 4, the Independent, Mirror, Sun, Express and Telegraph all stressed the hit to mortgage holders, with some warning of further increases if energy prices stay high. Savers stand to gain from higher returns. The pound had already been strong against a basket of currencies before the decision.

Sir Mark Rowley, commissioner of the Metropolitan Police, will warn that policing in England and Wales is "heading for failure" without urgent reform, the BBC reported. In a Friday speech he is proposing eight to ten regional forces, backed by hundreds of local commands and a new National Police Service, in what would be the biggest overhaul since the 1960s.

The Association of Police and Crime Commissioners said modernisation and a National Police Service make sense, but that cutting to nine mega-forces does not.
