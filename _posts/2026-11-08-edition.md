## 1. A Briton is among six people killed when a gunman described by Polish ministers as far-right opens fire in Krakow's main square, with three more bodies found at his home

A British national is among six people killed after a gunman opened fire in the Main Market Square of Krakow, Poland's second largest city, on Saturday afternoon, the BBC, Guardian and DW reported. Police said the attacker drove into the square shortly before 15:00 local time and fired at random people from his vehicle, moving to several spots in the square during an attack lasting about three minutes. Four women and two men were killed: three Polish women, one Briton and two people not yet identified. Sixteen people were injured, including five foreign nationals from Britain, Austria, Germany, Portugal and Russia, and two police officers, one critically.

The suspect, a 25-year-old Polish man, was shot by police and arrested after a chase and exchange of fire, and is in hospital after surgery. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said he was "fascinated by far-right ideologies" and held "ultranationalistic" views. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said three bodies found at the suspect's home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski were very likely his immediate family, and called the attack terrorism. The defence minister also called it an "act of terror". Poland is observing a period of mourning. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he was "shocked and saddened" and that the UK "stands in solidarity with the people of Poland". Jonathan Balshaw, from Ipswich, told the BBC he hid in a church as more than 20 shots were fired.

## 3. Andy Burnham pushes for closer EU ties ahead of a Brussels summit, as five European leaders back the UK's return to the EU electricity market and Michel Barnier warns against cherry picking

The Mirror reported that a 200-page draft deal removing checks on food and agricultural exports could add up to £5.1bn a year to the economy, and that an emissions agreement could add up to £3.1bn.

Michel Barnier, the EU's former Brexit negotiator, told the BBC's Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg that the UK could not "cherry pick" a single market deal and that access would require all four freedoms, including free movement of people.
