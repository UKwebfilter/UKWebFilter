## 1. Burnham says Russia interfered in Brexit and calls closer EU ties a duty

Andy Burnham has said it is "clear" that Russia interfered in the 2016 Brexit referendum, becoming the first UK prime minister to say so publicly, and is due to tell the Commons that rejecting closer ties with the EU would be a "dereliction of duty". In an interview with The Rest Is Politics podcast, released on Monday, he said that on arriving in Downing Street he asked whether Russia had interfered and received "the clear answer that, yes, there was." He did not give details of the activity, and said voters had not been "sufficiently" informed about foreign efforts to disrupt UK democracy. He also said he had discussed Russian disinformation with Germany's Chancellor Friedrich Merz.

Separately, Burnham will tell MPs it is time to "face up to the reality that Brexit has done more harm than good", arguing that a new EU deal could help tackle illegal immigration and boost growth, and that if he secured one "the vast majority of the public would expect us to take it". The Times reports he remains evasive about rejoining the EU without another referendum. The Express frames the Russia claim as "extraordinary"; Metro leads on his confirmation; Al Jazeera and Reuters note it is a first for a UK prime minister.

## 2. Deloitte helped in falsifying documents to evade tax on Abramovich's wealth

BBC Panorama reports that Deloitte, the world's biggest accountancy firm, helped in falsifying documents so that assets including superyachts, mansions and private jets worth $672m (£508m) vanished from Roman Abramovich's company accounts signed off in Cyprus, and that the firm appears to have advised on a scheme to evade a large tax bill through backdated paperwork. Barrister Andrew Mitchell KC told the programme the evidence showed "straightforward" tax evasion that "couldn't be more blatant". - [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/12/roman-abramovich-aides-paperwork-tax-records-suggest) (UK, commercial) · aides falsified paperwork to disguise assets and dodge tax, with the Tenenbaum angle.
  Focus 8: Centres the aides and the paperwork trail, a documentary frame that widens beyond Deloitte alone while staying on the documents.

Labour chair Bridget Phillipson called the plans a "clear sign of desperation and weakness". The Mirror reports Nigel Farage vowing to ban all asylum seekers for five years after a Reform poll plunge; the Express covers a Farage crackdown on countries refusing returns.
