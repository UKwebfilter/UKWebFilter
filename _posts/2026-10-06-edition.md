## 1. US pulls Fairford bombers after reported threats
The Pentagon has confirmed that all US bombers at RAF Fairford, including about a dozen B-1s used in the Iran conflict, returned to home stations in the United States over the weekend. President Trump said "some threats" prompted the move and, when asked, linked them to Iran. Secretary of State Marco Rubio had described the moves as routine rotations.

Iran denies involvement in a late-September incident near the base, in which several people were arrested and later released on bail. Anonymous US officials have described a drone plot ordered by Iran's Revolutionary Guard. That claim has not been independently confirmed.

**How it was framed**
- [Sky News](https://news.sky.com/story/us-removes-all-air-force-bombers-from-raf-fairford-after-suspected-terror-plot-13595162) (UK, commercial) leads on a "reported threat of possible Iranian drone attack."
- [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwj3413e5m1lo) (UK, public-service) keeps to Trump's "threat" wording and says it has not confirmed the drone claim.
- [The Sun](https://www.thesun.co.uk/news/40591706/trump-pulled-bombers-fears-iranian-drone-attack-raf-fairford/) (UK, tabloid) foregrounds fears of an Iranian drone attack and a "decoy" theory. It quotes experts who call the pullout a US "vote of no confidence."
- [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/05/no-10-says-raf-fairford-safe-us-withdraws-bombers-from-base) (UK, commercial) leads on Downing Street insisting the base is "safe." It quotes UK sources who doubt Iran could strike from 2,700 miles away.
- [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/us-pulls-all-bombers-key-uk-base-after-suspected-iran-linked-terror-plot) (US, commercial) headlines a "suspected Iran-linked terror plot," while [CNN](https://cnnespanol.cnn.com/2026/10/04/eeuu/retiran-bombarderos-base-aerea-fairford-trax) (US, commercial) describes an "unspecified" threat.
- [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261005/7888427bf9d3460a9e695801e48fc675/c.html) (China, state news agency) runs 「美媒：美军已将所有B-1轰炸机撤出…」 ("US media: US military has withdrawn all B-1 bombers…") and reports both the Iran link and Tehran's denial.
- [Tehran Times](https://www.tehrantimes.com/news/530784/US-scrambles-all-12-B-1-bombers-out-of-Britain-over-unexplained) (Iran, government-linked) cites "unexplained threats" and a "lack of concrete evidence." [RIA Novosti](https://ria.ru/20261006/ssha-2122534640.html) (Russia, state-owned) credits the drone claim to the Wall Street Journal and hedges it with «якобы» ("allegedly").

**Bias Spread: 7/10.** Outlets agree the bombers left. They split sharply on how certain the Iran drone link is, and on whether the move signals a UK security failure or a US precaution.

## 2. Tories pledge £10bn "Britannia Shield" air defence
The Conservatives have pledged about £10bn over ten years for an air-defence system called "Britannia Shield". Kemi Badenoch put the cost at £1bn a year. The plan covers radar, command systems, interceptor missiles and anti-drone equipment. The party says it would be funded within its plan to spend 3% of GDP on defence, through welfare savings and other cuts.

Labour's Wes Streeting called the Tory defence record "abysmal." Badenoch later said the system would not be the same as Israel's Iron Dome, a comparison the party's own materials had made. Critics question whether the cost is realistic.

**How it was framed**
- [Daily Mail](https://www.dailymail.com/news/article-16186345/Kemi-Badenoch-US-Fairford-UK-missile-drone-defences-Britannia-Shield.html) (UK, commercial) ties the pledge to Fairford, saying Badenoch blames the US pullout on gaps in UK air defence.
- [The Telegraph](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/politics/2026/10/05/tories-promise-iron-dome-uk/) (UK, commercial) headlines "Tories promise Iron Dome for UK," linking the plan to threats from Iran and Russian drones.
- [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/oct/06/badenoch-british-iron-dome-plan-tory-press-release) (UK, commercial) leads on Badenoch denying an Iron Dome plan, even though the party's press release described "in essence a British Iron Dome."
- [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cxvgdl5npl86o) (UK, public-service) explains the system's three parts neutrally and carries Labour's rebuttal.

**Bias Spread: 6/10.** The pledge itself is agreed. The split is over the "Iron Dome" branding and over whether Fairford proves the case for the plan.

## 3. King says his allowance won't fund Andrew's police challenge
Royal sources say King Charles's private allowance to Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor must not pay for his legal challenge to the search warrants used before his arrest in February. He was arrested on suspicion of misconduct in public office, in a case linked to the Epstein files, and denies wrongdoing. A High Court hearing is due on Thursday. Thames Valley Police said they would not comment while proceedings continue. The allowance is private royal income, not public money.

**How it was framed**
- The Telegraph's front page, as shown in the [BBC's review of today's papers](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw1m4l09mmr0o), splashes "King tells Andrew: I won't fund court fight."
- [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/oct/06/buckingham-palace-andrew-mountbatten-windsor-legal-action-police) (UK, commercial) reports that the Palace "will not fund" the action and details the warrants and the court process.
- [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c9ly0l028d1go) (UK, public-service) stresses that this is private rather than public money. It quotes a lawyer saying that overturning the warrants may not get seized material returned.

**Bias Spread: 3/10.** The core facts are shared. Coverage differs mainly in tone, from royal distancing to a legal explainer.

## 4. Flávio Bolsonaro and Lula head to Brazil runoff
With more than 99% of votes counted, Flávio Bolsonaro has about 47% of valid votes and President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva about 45%. Neither has a majority, so they face a runoff on 25 October. Flávio is the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is in prison after a conviction linked to a coup plot following the 2022 election. Pre-election polls had mostly favoured Lula. Claims that Flávio is now the favourite are analysis, not results.

**How it was framed**
- [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/05/flavio-bolsonaro-brazilian-presidency-election-shock-first-round-victory) (UK, commercial) uses "shock" and "far-right tsunami," and notes that first-round runners-up rarely win runoffs.
- [Fox News](https://noticias.foxnews.com/world/bolsonaro-wins-first-round-brazilian-presidential-election-trump-backed-candidates-continue-red-wave) (US, commercial, Spanish-language service) frames the result as a "Trump-backed… red wave" in which Bolsonaro "wins" the first round.
- [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/5/bolsonaro-favourite-to-win-what-brazils-first-round-of-election-tells-us) (Qatar, state-funded) calls Flávio the "favourite to win" and focuses on the stakes for the US and China.
- [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261005/d4ebeb412326420b9b02634097f02f0b/c.html) (China, state news agency) gives a neutral notice of the runoff, quoting both candidates, without a "far-right" label.
- [Folha de S.Paulo](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/poder/2026/10/flavio-lidera-onda-bolsonarista-ao-superar-o-proprio-pai-e-ir-ao-2o-turno-em-vantagem-contra-lula.shtml) (Brazil, commercial) runs 「Flávio lidera onda bolsonarista」 ("Flávio leads Bolsonarist wave") and highlights the right's gains in Congress.

**Bias Spread: 5/10.** The vote totals match across outlets. Tone ranges from "red wave" and "favourite" through neutral wire copy to "far-right shock."

## 5. Greens define Zionism as racism, and Israel bars their leader
The Green Party of England and Wales voted 1,022 to 813 at its conference for a motion that defines Zionism as racism and backs a single democratic state in "historic Palestine." Leader Zack Polanski, who is Jewish, did not vote. He later said no one should be excluded because of their faith.

Israel condemned the motion, and Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar barred Polanski from entering the country. Labour MPs referred the motion to the Equality and Human Rights Commission and to Sussex Police, who say they are reviewing it. The European Greens distanced themselves from the vote.

**How it was framed**
- [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cjy56yxxwy3po) (UK, public-service) leads on Polanski saying he "won't accept exclusion," alongside the Labour referrals and the European Greens' response.
- [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/4/israel-lashes-out-as-uks-green-party-formally-defines-zionism-as-racism) (Qatar, state-funded) headlines "Israel lashes out," quotes President Herzog calling the motion an "antisemitic lie," and adds a Green deputy's points about the war in Gaza.
- [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/r1vaqyxsge) (Israel, commercial) leads on Sa'ar banning Polanski and on the foreign ministry calling the motion antisemitic and a legitimisation of terrorism.

**Bias Spread: 7/10.** The vote and the ban are agreed. Framing divides sharply between antisemitism and terrorism on one side and Gaza, free speech and party inclusion on the other.
