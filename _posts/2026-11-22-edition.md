## 1. Theresa May tells Andy Burnham 'Brexit is done' and to move on, as Labour grandees warn against another referendum and the row over EU student fees rumbles on

Former prime minister Theresa May has told Andy Burnham to "move on" from Brexit, saying in a BBC interview to be broadcast on Sunday that she is "aghast" he could be considering rejoining the European Union. "Brexit is done. Just accept Brexit and move on," she told Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg, and accused the prime minister of wanting to "pat people on the head" by suggesting voters got it wrong in 2016.

The intervention follows Friday's Brussels summit, where Burnham agreed deals on food trade, emissions and youth mobility and said he wanted to "brainstorm" options for closer ties. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the BBC on Saturday that "all the options will be on the table" and that the deal would go ahead regardless of a separate dispute over tuition fees for EU students, which both sides have given six months to resolve. Legislation for the package is due in Parliament this week.

Other senior figures weighed in. Sir Tony Blair told the Sunday Times the UK should only rejoin if the EU reforms and allows more innovation on technology. Alan Johnson, who launched Labour's 2016 Remain campaign, told the Sunday Mirror closer ties made sense but another referendum "would be very damaging", and Lord Blunkett told the Sun on Sunday the electorate was not ready for bolder integration.

The Mail on Sunday reported claims from Lord Frost and Labour MP Graham Stringer that the EU would not implement the agreements until Britain gave ground on fees, which the government denies. The Conservatives said linking to the EU emissions trading system would add £281 a year to household costs.

## 3. England beat New Zealand 37-12 at Twickenham for a record margin of victory over the All Blacks, after former captain Lewis Moody, who has motor neurone disease, joined the team to face the haka

England beat New Zealand 37-12 at Twickenham on Saturday in the Nations Championship, their biggest winning margin over the All Blacks and, by margin, New Zealand's heaviest defeat by any northern hemisphere side. England scored five tries, with Henry Slade, Jack van Poortvliet and Ben Earl crossing in the first half and Ollie Chessum adding a fourth soon after the restart. The hosts led 19-0 after 36 minutes.

Before kick-off, former England captain Lewis Moody, who has motor neurone disease, joined the players to face the haka. Moody, who won 71 caps and was part of the 2003 World Cup-winning side, presented the shirts the day before. "I think he and his family gave us that extra lift today," captain Maro Itoje said, while playing down the importance of how a team responds to the haka. "The most important thing is how you start the game."

England lost winger Tommy Freeman to a thigh problem before the match, with Cadan Murley starting on the left wing. The result is England's fifth win in a row, after victories over Fiji, Argentina, Australia and Japan, and the first time since 2003 they have beaten New Zealand in back-to-back Tests. It also follows the All Blacks' defeat by Wales last week. "We don't want this to be our ceiling," Itoje said. Elsewhere, Ireland beat South Africa 20-17 and Australia edged Wales.

## 6. Cop31 hosts publish a new draft deal overnight to rescue the climate talks in Turkey, acknowledging a past pledge to move away from fossil fuels without restating it, as the summit enters a second day of overtime

Negotiators at the Cop31 UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, were due to reconvene on Sunday morning to decide whether to approve a new draft agreement published in the early hours, as the talks ran into a second day of overtime. The two-week summit was due to end on Friday.

The new text, produced by co-hosts Turkey and Australia after talks with nearly 200 delegations, still contains no specific reference to "transitioning away from fossil fuels". But it does acknowledge the commitment countries made to do so three years ago and, for the first time, recognises that the world is on track to miss the goal of limiting warming to 1.5C. An opening draft on Monday had called for a faster phase-out, but Thursday's version dropped every mention of coal, oil and gas after objections from Saudi Arabia and other producers, prompting threats of a walkout.

Cop31 President Murat Kurum, Turkey's environment minister, told a late-night session on Saturday: "We believe that we have put forward a text which all the parties can agree upon." EU climate commissioner Wopke Hoekstra had warned that without adequate climate action "there's no deal", while the UK's chief negotiator, Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh, said her team was still working for agreement. The Guardian reported that about 130 countries wanted the fossil fuel wording restored. The Africa Group is expected to be disappointed that the text sets no figure for adaptation finance. If there is no consensus, talks could be suspended until next year.
