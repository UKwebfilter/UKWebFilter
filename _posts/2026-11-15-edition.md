## 1. The government says it will consider changing the age of consent after a woman waives her anonymity to allege Russell Brand raped her when she was 16 and he was 30

The government has said it will consider calls to change the age of consent after a woman waived her right to anonymity to allege that Russell Brand raped her when she was 16. Mia Latter, now 37, told the Sunday Times she met Brand, then 30 and working at MTV, in Leicester Square on her way home from a shopping trip. She alleges he told her to keep their three-month relationship secret, referred to her as "the child" in front of his entourage, and orally raped her towards the end of it.

Latter was one of six women who made allegations in the 2023 Times, Sunday Times and Channel 4 Dispatches investigation, under the pseudonym "Alice". Brand denied those allegations when they were first reported. The BBC noted that none of the women in that investigation were complainants at his recent trial. On Monday Brand was sentenced to 10 and a half years for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, and was acquitted on other counts.

Latter is campaigning for a "staggered" age of consent that would make relationships between adults and 16 and 17-year-olds illegal, while not criminalising teenagers together. Louise Haigh, the de facto deputy prime minister, told the paper that changing the law "would be a massive step" but "deserves proper consideration and scrutiny". Under current UK law, adults of any age can legally have sex with 16 and 17-year-olds. Latter said she would not continue with the criminal process so she could focus on her campaign.

## 3. The government expands its subsidised jobs scheme for long-term unemployed young people across Great Britain, as Alan Milburn prepares to publish his review into nearly a million 'Neets'

A subsidised job scheme for long-term unemployed 18 to 24-year-olds is to be rolled out across Great Britain, Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden has announced. After a trial in six areas with some of the highest youth unemployment, including Greater Manchester and South West Wales, the Jobs Guarantee will be expanded nationally, the Mirror reported. From April 2027 it will open to people who have claimed Universal Credit for 12 months rather than 18. The government funds up to 25 hours of paid work a week for six months, with an aim of helping 90,000 young people by 2029.

"No young person should be left waiting on a scrapheap for an opportunity," McFadden said. The announcement comes days before Alan Milburn publishes his review into the almost one million young people not in education, employment or training, known as Neets. He will present it to Andy Burnham's Cabinet on Tuesday.

Milburn told the BBC there should be a permanent "opportunity guarantee" of a job or training, set in law. The BBC reported that he will also argue for stopping under-25s claiming the main sickness and disability benefits, and for shifting spending on young people from benefits, currently 25 times what is spent on employment support, to a ratio of two to one. He accepted openly that this would mean a significant number of young people losing benefits or facing cuts. The Telegraph reported that Burnham has refused to commit to implementing all of the reforms.

## 4. New cold health alerts come into force for parts of England from Sunday night after temperatures fell to minus 7C, with ice, frost and hill snow forecast for the week ahead

The UK Health Security Agency has issued a new round of cold health alerts for parts of England, in force from 9pm on Sunday until 9am on Thursday. The BBC said they cover the north-east and north-west of England and Yorkshire and the Humber, while the Guardian listed the north-west, the West Midlands and Yorkshire and the Humber. The alerts mean the weather is "likely to have minor impacts on health and social care services", including more use of healthcare and a greater risk to life for vulnerable people.

The warnings follow a bitter start to Saturday, when minus 7.2C was recorded at South Newington in Oxfordshire, the lowest temperature of the autumn so far, according to the BBC. Sunday was forecast to be milder, with temperatures of around 2C to 6C for most places but widespread rain, possible hail and thunderstorms in parts of Wales and southern England, and the risk of localised flooding from leaves blocking drains.

Colder air is expected to return from Monday, bringing frost, fog and light snow on northern hills. A Met Office meteorologist told the Press Association there was potential for "some disruptive ice" on Sunday night and Monday morning. Paul Coleman of the UKHSA said more alerts could be expected as winter goes on and urged people to check on friends, family and neighbours aged 65 and over or with health conditions, to heat the rooms they use most and to wear warm clothes.

## 5. A former Met Police detective investigated for sexually exploiting vulnerable victims lied about the death of his wife in a Barbados hotel room, a BBC Panorama investigation reports

A former Metropolitan Police detective who was investigated for sexually exploiting vulnerable crime victims later lied about the death of his wife in a Barbados hotel room, BBC Panorama has reported. Warren Arter met his wife Rebekah after she reported domestic abuse to the Met in 2006, when he investigated her case. He then pursued a relationship with her and, the BBC says, abused her for more than a decade.

Rebekah Arter died in June 2024, aged 47, after Arter rang hotel reception to say she had collapsed. Her cause of death is still not known, and a UK coroner recorded an open verdict. Panorama says hotel staff, police sources and Arter's own messages show he gave conflicting accounts, telling staff and her family variously that she had choked, fallen and banged her head, or that he had woken up and found her dead.

The programme reports failures by Barbados authorities to preserve evidence and interview hotel staff, and says vital forensic tests were not taken. Despite a request from her family, the Met did not send detectives to the island; it told the BBC this was because no allegation of criminality had been made. Four days after her death a senior Met officer was briefed about the case in light of Arter's alleged offending. Arter had been suspended for seven years and investigated by the police watchdog over his conduct towards eight alleged victims, but was never convicted. He took his own life in HMP Wandsworth days after returning from Barbados and being arrested for allegedly abusing another woman. Rebekah's family say the Met failed her.

## 7. SpaceX catches its Starship spacecraft with the launch tower's mechanical arms after 14 orbits of Earth, the first recovery of the upper stage from orbit, a day after losing the booster

SpaceX has caught its Starship spacecraft with the giant mechanical "chopstick" arms of its launch tower in Texas, the first time the upper stage has been recovered after a flight to orbit. The 52-metre spacecraft returned on Saturday after a mission of more than 22 hours in which it orbited Earth 14 times and deployed 26 Starlink satellites. It flipped upright, restarted three of its six Raptor engines to slow to a near hover and was gripped by the arms moments before shutdown, CBS reported.

"Can you believe it? Look at her! Baby, she's back!" SpaceX commentator Dan Huot shouted. Nasa Administrator Jared Isaacman called it "almost magic". SpaceX said everything on the spacecraft, including its heat shield, appeared to work well, and that the catch demonstrated the vehicle's full reusability.

The 15th test flight had been planned as a first double catch, but the Super Heavy booster was diverted shortly after Friday's launch and exploded in the Gulf of Mexico. SpaceX said the booster's guidance system detected it was not in the right position for a catch and triggered the detour, and Elon Musk said it was "slightly low on fuel". Engineers are still investigating. The company had already caught boosters three times before. Nasa is paying billions of dollars for Starship to land Artemis astronauts on the Moon later this decade, and the Guardian noted it was only the second time Starship had reached orbit.

## 8. The US transport secretary says a router installed during a copper-to-fibre upgrade by a contractor triggered Friday's nationwide ground stop, as flight disruption continued into Saturday

US Transport Secretary Sean Duffy says Friday's nationwide ground stop, the longest since the 11 September 2001 attacks, began during work by a contractor to switch telecommunications lines at a remote Federal Aviation Administration facility from copper wire to fibre optic cable. He said a router being worked on started flooding other air traffic control systems, causing communication problems with planes, and the government shut the airspace for safety.
