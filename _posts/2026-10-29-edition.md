## 1. Healey's first Budget hands £100 to 20 million households and raises taxes on banks and landlords, as forecasters warn of a grim squeeze

Chancellor John Healey raised taxes on banks and landlords to fund a £100 winter payment for 20 million households, in the first Budget since Andy Burnham became prime minister, The Guardian reported. The one-off payment goes to households in council tax bands A to D in England, with equivalent funding for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, the BBC said.

The BBC's summary listed standing charges removed from household gas bills, the VAT cut on electricity extended to July, the fuel duty freeze extended to July and import taxes lifted on more than 100 food products. The surcharge banks pay on profits will rise from 3% to 6% for 15 months, and an extra 2% tax will apply to income from savings and property rental from April. The Guardian said the Office for Budget Responsibility warned of a "grim picture" for living standards, with "virtually no growth" in disposable income, and that the property tax change risked a steady rise in rents. Healey later told the BBC there would be a reduction in the welfare bill before the next election. The Daily Mail led on middle earners facing a fall in living standards, while the Telegraph called it a tax raid on savers.

## 6. A high-speed train crash in central Spain kills at least 68 people, with the transport minister blaming a computer failure and an "inexplicable" control centre decision

Iryo said its train was "struck from behind" by a Renfe train carrying 397 passengers. Survivors described being thrown into the air and breaking windows to escape, and one carriage caught fire.
