## 1. Ofgem confirms energy bills will rise 26% from January as gas prices surge with the Iran war, lifting a typical annual bill to £2,166, while Andy Burnham rules out further help this winter

Energy bills for millions of households in England, Scotland and Wales will rise by 26% on 1 January after the regulator Ofgem raised its price cap by £443. A household using a typical amount of energy and paying by direct debit would see its annual bill go from £1,723 to £2,166 if the new rate lasted a year, the first time the cap has passed £2,000 since the winter after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Ofgem said the increase was driven entirely by wholesale costs, mainly because of the Iran war, with wholesale gas prices up 33% over the three months used to set the cap.

The cap limits unit prices rather than total bills. Electricity will rise from 26.32p to 32.69p per kilowatt-hour and gas from 7.97p to 10.96p. About a third of households on fixed deals are not affected until their terms end. The government will pay £100 from 4 January to households in council tax bands A to D, on top of the £150 warm home discount for about six million households on benefits.

Energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said she knew people were "deeply concerned". In the Commons, Andy Burnham said the rise would "cause concern to many people" but that the £100 payment would give "some breathing space"; Downing Street said there were no plans for further action this winter. Kemi Badenoch said "hope does not pay their energy bills", Sir Ed Davey called for a windfall tax on energy firms, and Citizens Advice warned of "a brutal winter". Cornwall Insight expects bills to fall only slightly in April.

## 3. Andy Taylor, the Tynemouth-born guitarist who co-wrote Duran Duran hits including Rio, Save A Prayer and Girls On Film, dies at home aged 65 after an eight-year battle with prostate cancer

Andy Taylor, the original guitarist of Duran Duran, has died aged 65. In a joint statement, the band and his family said he had fought "a long and courageous battle with prostate cancer" and "passed away peacefully at home, surrounded by his family, on Monday 23 November". He was diagnosed with stage four metastatic prostate cancer in 2018 and disclosed it publicly in 2022, when Duran Duran were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

Born in Tynemouth, the son of a fisherman, Taylor taught himself guitar, bass and drums and was playing professionally at 13. He answered a music paper advert for a "live-wire guitarist" and auditioned for the band in Birmingham in April 1980. He co-wrote some of their biggest hits, including Rio, Save A Prayer and Girls On Film, and played on others such as Hungry Like The Wolf and The Reflex, as the group rose to global fame with the New Romantic movement and the early MTV era. He also worked with Robert Palmer, Rod Stewart and Belinda Carlisle.

His wife of more than 40 years, Tracey, said he "was the rock of our family", adding: "To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather." His former bandmates said that from the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East "he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music". The Mirror used the news to set out the warning signs of prostate cancer, citing Prostate Cancer UK figures showing diagnoses have risen by 42% over the past decade.

## 5. Poundland collapses into administration weeks before Christmas, putting about 11,000 jobs at risk, after its US owner Gordon Brothers rejected last-ditch bids and the chain is expected to be broken up

Poundland has collapsed into administration, putting about 11,000 jobs at risk weeks before Christmas. AlixPartners has been appointed to run the retailer, which has about 600 stores in the UK and Ireland, after a sales process "did not identify a viable solvent solution for the business as a whole". Shops and distribution centres will stay open while administrators look at selling the business in whole or in part, and the chain is expected to be broken up, with some stores sold to rivals and others closed.

Poundland boss Barry Williams told staff the board had "no alternative" and acted with "sincere sadness and intense regret". He said the US owner, Gordon Brothers, had removed "all borrowing facilities and access to our bank accounts" on 27 October, and that adequate funds had not been released since. Gordon Brothers, which bought the chain for a nominal £1 from Pepco last year, said it had "explored every avenue" to sell the whole company. The Guardian reported that the owner rejected at least two last-ditch bids, and that the workforce is about 2,000 smaller than a month ago, with up to 30,000 jobs across suppliers and other businesses potentially affected.

The shopworkers' union Usdaw called it "devastating news" and accused the owner of a "disgraceful" failure to engage with staff. Andy Burnham said Poundland was "part of the fabric of high streets" and that the timing would be "deeply concerning for workers and their families". Kelvin Blake, who has worked at the firm for 25 years, told the BBC: "My income's gone." Figures for jobs at risk vary slightly between outlets, from 10,000 to 11,000.

## 6. The US unveils proposed new tariffs of up to 40% on China, the EU and 12 other economies after a trade investigation, drawing defiance from Brazil and charges of 'political manipulation' from Beijing

The US Trade Representative has announced a fresh round of proposed tariffs on many of America's biggest trading partners, following an investigation into their trade practices launched in March after the Supreme Court ruled that Donald Trump's earlier "Liberation Day" tariffs were illegal. The new levies would apply to goods from China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand and Bangladesh. The UK is not on the list reported by the BBC.

China faces the highest rate of up to 40%, followed by Vietnam at up to 35%, with others facing 20% or 25%, the BBC reported, though some rates will be reduced by caps Trump has already agreed.
