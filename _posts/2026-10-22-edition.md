## 1. Critical flaw in the PHP web language forces websites offline around the world as the UK checks its own exposure

Websites around the world have been taken offline after two critical security flaws were found in PHP, a programming language used by roughly 70% of websites. The UK government said it was working to identify any potential exposure in its online services and urged "all organisations, private and public, large and small" to follow official guidance, the BBC reported.

The flaws, dubbed PHP2Shell, were found with the help of artificial intelligence by security firms Calif and Wiz in August, DW and Al Jazeera reported. Details leaked on social media on Tuesday, a day before a fix was released on Wednesday afternoon. Wiz said they ranked among the most severe vulnerabilities ever discovered in PHP. The US National Security Agency and the UK's National Cyber Security Centre advised organisations that could not apply the fix immediately to consider taking systems offline.

Government sites in Finland, the Netherlands, Belgium, France and Germany were among those temporarily shut down, the Guardian reported, and the Wikimedia Foundation took down several systems as a precaution. Edinburgh council's website and pages for several BBC programmes also went offline, though no link to PHP had been confirmed. Amazon said it had detected exploitation attempts before the warning was made public, and Cloudflare said it was seeing early attacks. Experts warned of a wave of intrusions now that the fix is public.

## 2. Government approves the Jackdaw gas field as the energy secretary signals she may reject the much larger Rosebank oilfield

The government has approved production from the Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea, 150 miles east of Aberdeen, with ministers saying first gas could flow by the end of this month. Operator Adura, a joint venture between Shell and Equinor, says Jackdaw will provide about 6% of UK gas production, enough for 1.4 million homes, the BBC reported. The government said the field would protect hundreds of jobs and add £2.9bn to the economy over its 11-year life, and announced a new energy wealth fund.

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said the decision was compatible with the UK's climate commitments but told BBC Radio Scotland it would not lower bills this winter. Prime Minister Andy Burnham asked her to look at scrapping standing charges on gas, Channel 4 News reported. Kemi Badenoch accused him of dithering and called for the ban on new licences to be repealed. The Green Party called the approval "climate vandalism", DW reported.

The Guardian reported that Fahnbulleh is minded to reject the much larger Rosebank oilfield off Shetland, telling the paper that one was a gasfield for the domestic market and the other an oilfield mainly for export. She could still be overruled by Burnham, and a final decision is expected before the end of the year.

## 3. Offenders who refuse to attend sentencing will face cell confinement and lose prison visits under new powers

Judges in England and Wales will be given new powers to keep offenders confined to their cells and to block prison visits if they refuse to attend their sentencing hearings, the Ministry of Justice has announced. The sanctions, due to come into force next year, build on an existing law under which criminals who refuse to attend can be fined or given up to two more years for contempt of court, the BBC reported.

Under the new measures, offenders could be confined to their cells for up to three weeks, lose social visits for nearly a month and have privileges such as gym access or a television removed for up to 12 weeks, the Daily Mail reported. Visits from prisoners' children would be exempt, and shorter periods would apply to offenders aged 18 to 20. The rules were due to be put before MPs on Thursday, the Daily Mirror reported.

Prisons Minister Catherine McKinnell said criminals "should not be able to pick and choose when they face justice". The families of Olivia Pratt-Korbel, Sabina Nessa, Zara Aleena and Jan Mustafa, whose killers did not attend sentencing, had campaigned for change. Pia Sinha of the Prison Reform Trust asked for evidence the sanctions would work and warned they would punish prisoners' relatives.

## 4. MPs warn of glaring gaps in UK defences in the High North that leave it open to Russian threats

The UK has "glaring gaps" in its ability to defend the High North, leaving undersea cables, energy supplies and Atlantic supply routes exposed to potential Russian attacks, the Commons Defence Committee has warned. The MPs pointed to a shortage of Royal Navy warships and submarines, a "shockingly low availability" of submarines and a temporary loss of the ability to operate them beneath Arctic ice, the BBC reported.

The committee highlighted the slow deployment of HMS Dragon to Cyprus this spring, declining numbers of frigates and reports that all five Astute-class attack submarines were in dock this summer, the Guardian reported. The navy has five Type 23 frigates and six Type 45 destroyers in service, just over half its requirement for 19 vessels, the Daily Mirror reported. MPs called for a plan to put more warships and submarines at sea by 2030 and warned of critical gaps if the United States were not involved in countering Russian aggression.

Committee chair Tan Dhesi said the UK "does not currently have all the capabilities to operate successfully in the high north". The Ministry of Defence said it was "clear-eyed" about the threat from Russia and was investing heavily to protect undersea cables. Conservatives called for higher defence spending, the Daily Express reported.

## 6. US orders some diplomats to leave Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks kill three people at an airport and an oilfield

The US State Department has ordered non-emergency government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia, citing escalating attacks by Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthis on civilian airports and other infrastructure.
