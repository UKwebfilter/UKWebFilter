David Walliams, the comedian, former Britain's Got Talent judge and best-selling children's author, has been accused by multiple women of using his power and celebrity status to prey on young women and teenagers for sex. The claims come from a joint investigation by the Guardian and ITV News that heard from 30 sources with first-hand accounts of his conduct. Two women said they first met him when they were 15 and that he groomed them, and one woman alleged that he sexually assaulted her at his home when she was 15, the Daily Express reported. He is also accused of abusing his position at Britain's Got Talent and at his former publisher HarperCollins.

Walliams, 55, denies any wrongdoing. In a letter published by the Guardian he said he was "not a sexual predator" and had not "broken the law", but accepted that at times he had "behaved thoughtlessly". The claims could be published after the high court rejected his bid for a lifelong injunction, in a hearing told he had twice tried to take his own life, and his lawyers said he would not appeal. The Metropolitan Police said it had received no related reports but would "carefully assess" any it receives. Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy called the claims the "latest in a long line of reported abuses of power", Downing Street called them "deeply troubling", and the story dominated Wednesday's front pages.

## 2. The US charges three alleged Iran-backed operatives over the foiled plot at RAF Fairford, saying one directed it from a New York jail

The US Justice Department has charged three men it describes as Iran-backed operatives over a string of plots in Europe and the US, including the foiled attack on RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire in September, where American bombers were stationed. Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, a dual Iranian-Iraqi national, Iranian Mohsen Mohajeri and Iraqi Mohammad Ali al-Hassani were named in an eight-count indictment unsealed in Manhattan on Tuesday. Only al-Saadi is in custody. Prosecutors allege he planned the attack from a New York jail, passing coded messages through calls to his mother and sister, the Daily Mail reported. Al-Hassani is accused of directing and paying associates in the UK to watch the base, obtain vans and barrels of fuel and research chemical accelerants and shrapnel, the Guardian said.

The department said seven of 18 attacks claimed in Europe this spring by a group calling itself HAYI took place in the UK, including the near-fatal stabbing of two Jewish men in London. Five men were arrested near the base on 27 September after a farmer reported vans blocking a road, and the BBC said seven men have now been arrested in the UK over the incident. Iran has strongly denied involvement. Separately, US officials confirmed that more than a dozen aircraft were moved out of RAF Mildenhall this week amid a reported threat from Iran, Fox News said.

## 3. A senior US Army soldier is arrested in Gloucestershire on suspicion of spying for Russia, in what police call a significant arrest

A senior soldier in the US Army has been arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia, counter-terrorism police said. The 41-year-old was arrested near Gloucester on Tuesday under sections one and three of the National Security Act 2023, which cover obtaining or disclosing protected information and assisting a foreign intelligence service. The Metropolitan Police said "the country to which the allegations relate is Russia", NBC News reported. He remains in custody at a London police station, and officers were carrying out searches at two locations in Gloucestershire.

The BBC and the Guardian said he was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, the headquarters of Nato's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, and a senior Nato military official confirmed to NBC News that the arrested service member was assigned to the corps. US Army counterintelligence command said the allegations related to a "senior soldier", the Guardian reported. Police said the case was not linked to any other ongoing investigation. Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans called it a "significant arrest" that came from close collaboration between UK and US authorities, adding that the allegations were serious and the investigation "very much ongoing". FBI director Kash Patel said the allegations of espionage were "of the utmost concern" to the United States and its allies, and that the bureau had "no tolerance for anyone who breaks their oath".

## 5. A US judge lets Donald Trump's $10bn defamation lawsuit against the BBC over an edited Panorama speech go ahead, as Trump hails a huge victory

A federal judge in Florida has allowed Donald Trump's $10bn (£7.5bn) defamation lawsuit against the BBC to go ahead, rejecting the broadcaster's bid to have it dismissed. The case concerns a Panorama documentary, Trump: A Second Chance, that spliced together two parts of a speech Trump gave before the riot at the US Capitol on 6 January 2021, remarks the Guardian said were nearly 55 minutes apart. The BBC has apologised for the edit, saying it gave a mistaken impression. Judge Jeffrey Kuntz dismissed a second claim brought under Florida's unfair trade practices law, the BBC reported, and did not rule on the merits. "Whether President Trump can prove these claims and establish he sustained damages at summary judgment or trial is left for another day," he wrote.

The judge said that "on its face, the editing may independently establish the plausibility of actual malice", and that he was not persuaded by the BBC's argument that it was ordinary editing to condense a long speech, the Guardian reported. Trump called the ruling "a Huge Victory against Fake News BBC" on Truth Social, describing the corporation as "a terrible abuser of the truth", the Daily Mirror said. The BBC had warned of a "chilling effect" on reporting of public figures. A BBC spokesperson said it was surprised by the decision but respected the court's authority, remained confident in its position and would continue to respond through the proper legal process.

## 6. The US House blocks Thomas Massie's bid to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war by 211 votes to 204, as Donald Trump hails an exoneration

The US House of Representatives has shelved an attempt to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war, voting 211-204 on Tuesday to refer Republican congressman Thomas Massie's articles of impeachment to the Republican-led Judiciary Committee. The move effectively kills the effort before Democrats take control of Congress in January. Massie was the only Republican to vote against the referral, alongside all the Democrats who voted, while 18 members, 11 Democrats and seven Republicans, missed the vote, CBS News reported.

Massie's eight articles accuse Hegseth of waging war in Iran without congressional authorisation, ignoring laws meant to limit civilian casualties and illegally directing the seizure of Venezuela's Nicolás Maduro, the Daily Mail said. In his first Pentagon news briefing in six months, Hegseth called the effort a "farce", "sabotage" and a "political spectacle". Donald Trump celebrated on Truth Social, calling Massie "a major LOSER" and the outcome the "EXONERATION of a Great American Patriot". The vote came a week after Republicans lost control of both chambers in the midterm elections, losses several Republicans have blamed on the Iran war, ABC News reported. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said: "This is not the end of it. It's just the beginning." Massie, who lost his primary after breaking with the president, said he would now try to force a vote on a war powers resolution.
