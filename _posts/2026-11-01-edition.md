King Charles will speak with "unflinching honesty" and "sincere sorrow" about the lasting pain of the transatlantic slave trade when he addresses Commonwealth leaders in Antigua on Tuesday, Buckingham Palace has said, the BBC reported. The speech at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting comes on the final leg of his nine-day Caribbean tour.

A Palace spokesperson said the King "will not shy away from confronting the most shameful aspects of the past during this visit". The BBC said the Palace was told in advance of Prime Minister Andy Burnham's opinion piece on Friday, in which he said he was "open" to a formal apology, and that the King has not changed his speech. The Daily Mirror said the King is under increasing pressure after Burnham went further than any predecessor, while the Palace has consistently said the King would not offer an apology himself. The Independent and The Sun both said no apology is expected from the King on the tour. The British government has never formally apologised for slavery or offered reparations.

## 5. Chancellor John Healey suggests younger workers may not get the full adult minimum wage before the next election, risking a Labour manifesto promise

Chancellor John Healey has suggested that younger workers might not receive the same minimum wage as older workers before the next election, potentially breaking a Labour manifesto promise, the BBC reported. In 2024, Labour said it would remove "discriminatory age bands" and end the gap between the adult rate and pay for 18 to 20-year-olds.

Asked in an interview with Laura Kuenssberg, to be broadcast on Sunday, whether the rates would be equal by the election, Healey said: "We'll take one step at a time. There is no question about whether or if, it's a question of when, it's a question of how fast." He said he wanted to "look hard" at the trade-offs between jobs and wages and to bring business and unions with him, describing a million young people out of work as "a moral shame on this nation".

In last week's Budget, younger workers were given the same increase as older workers, leaving a gap. Pay for 18 to 20-year-olds will rise to £11.27, while over-20s will get at least £13.21 an hour, the Daily Mirror said. The BBC said the shift is likely to anger unions and some on the left. Healey also signalled he was ready to make cuts to some departments to get debt falling.

## 6. Rioters loot shops in Frankfurt as youth-led cost of living protests spread to Germany, while hundreds of thousands march again in France

Rioters looted shops in central Frankfurt late on Saturday and clashes broke out with police there and in Berlin, Cologne and Hamburg after largely peaceful mass protests over the cost of living, the BBC reported. The demonstrations were organised by the campaign group Gen Z regelt, meaning "Gen Z takes charge", against underfunding in schools, the high cost of living, spending cuts and military service. Authorities reported up to 18,000 protesters, about half the organisers' estimate. In France, the interior ministry said 320,000 people took part in demonstrations, including 70,000 in Paris, DW reported. Police fired tear gas in Paris and Lyon, fires burned in Marseille, and officers were caught in flames from incendiary devices. More than 290 people were arrested across France. ## 7. The FBI says North Korea was behind the record $6.3bn Binance hack, as the exchange says it has covered 90% of the losses

The FBI has said North Korea was responsible for the theft of $6.3bn (£4.7bn) in crypto assets from Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, the BBC reported. It named the North Korean state-backed group TraderTraitor, which The Guardian said is believed to be part of the Lazarus Group, accused of hacking Sony Pictures in 2014. North Korea has always denied involvement in such attacks.

Binance said on Saturday it had replaced 90% of the stolen assets using its own funds, including its emergency user fund and corporate treasury, and that the remaining 10% were less liquid tokens that may take longer to buy. "Binance will absorb the financial impact of these stolen funds, not our users," the company said. It said it was on track to reopen withdrawals on Sunday.

Al Jazeera said the FBI observed the hackers converting stolen assets into Ethereum and expected further laundering, and urged firms to block transactions linked to the addresses involved. The same group was blamed for a $1.5bn theft from Bybit in 2025. Co-chief executive Richard Teng said the breach was a "complex attack" involving a compromised laptop and software designed to delete evidence. Binance said about $3bn of stolen assets had been frozen.
