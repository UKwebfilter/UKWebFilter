## 1. Polanski vows to stay on as Green leader as pressure mounts after by-election defeat

Zack Polanski has said he will stay on as leader of the Green Party of England and Wales after finishing second to Labour in Thursday's Holborn and St Pancras by-election, where Labour's Sagal Abdi-Wali won with 12,292 votes to his 8,928. The chair of the Jewish Greens, Vivien Lichtenstein, called on Friday for him to resign, saying it was a "big mistake" for him to have missed the party conference debate on a motion, since passed, that declares Zionism a form of racism and calls for Israel to be replaced by a single Palestinian state. Former leader Caroline Lucas had already criticised him for a lack of leadership over the vote. The Guardian reports that another member of the Jewish Greens said Lichtenstein did not speak for the majority of the group and that a vote of no confidence in her had been tabled.

Polanski was also challenged on Radio 4's Today programme over whether Israel has a right to exist. Saturday's iWeekend front page says his leadership is "now in doubt", while the Times, in a leader, suggests the party's "flirtation with extremism will inevitably backfire" and the Mail's Dan Hodges calls the result "the most important rejection of anti-semitism since Cable Street", according to the BBC's review of the papers.

## 2. King pays Andrew's £1.5m repair bill for Royal Lodge

King Charles has paid the £1.5m bill owed by his brother, Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, for the poor condition of his former home at Royal Lodge in Windsor, the BBC reports. It is understood the money comes from the King's private income and covers what the former prince owed his former landlords, the Crown Estate, on leaving the 30-room house. The total penalty for "dilapidations" was £1.8m, reduced by the roughly £300,000 he would have received for surrendering his lease early. The Crown Estate says it has given no money to Mountbatten-Windsor at the end of the lease and has now received the net £1.5m it was owed.

Mountbatten-Windsor left Royal Lodge in February amid revelations about his links to Jeffrey Epstein, which he has consistently denied, and the King has since used his own money to pay for his upkeep and accommodation at Sandringham. The story leads Saturday's Sun ("The king & I.O.U") and Mirror ("King's £1.5m bailout for Andrew"), and the Mail runs a separate exclusive on friends urging the former prince to challenge the King.

## 3. Healey faces pressure from business and his own Cabinet before the Budget

With the first Budget of Andy Burnham's government approaching, several of Saturday's papers lead on pressure on Chancellor John Healey. The Times reports that the prime minister and the chancellor rejected a plea from Health Secretary Yvette Cooper for more money to fund an NHS pay deal, writing to her jointly, in what the paper calls a "highly unusual move", to say any increase must come from the health service's existing budget. The Telegraph reports that Jason Tarry, chairman of John Lewis and Waitrose, warned that a "tax raid" on large stores would "only harm our high streets", as Burnham has signalled the government could raise a tax on warehouses to help fund tax cuts for businesses with "social benefit", like pubs and restaurants.

In the Daily Mail, Marks & Spencer chief executive Stuart Machin urges Healey to "take the handcuffs off business" and reverse "the mistakes of the last two Budgets", and the Mail also reports a City warning that a bank tax could drive investors away. From the other direction, the Independent reports Labour MPs pressing Healey to bring in a private jet tax on billionaires.

## 5. Northern Ireland Secretary bans the Drumcree parade

Northern Ireland Secretary Sir Chris Bryant has said he will ban the contentious Orange Order parade at Drumcree, after a stand-off of almost two weeks between Orangemen and nationalist residents of the Garvaghy Road in Portadown. He said the chief constable had advised him that "it would not be possible to enable the procession to happen without a major intervention which would carry an unacceptable risk of serious injury, disorder and a wider contagion effect across Northern Ireland."

The Parades Commission had ruled that the march on Sunday 27 September could go ahead with restrictions, but it did not happen because of a large protest on the Garvaghy Road; the law allows a secretary of state to overturn a commission determination. Sinn Féin and the SDLP welcomed the decision, while DUP leader Gavin Robinson said Bryant now needed to "explain what his government intends to do to restore confidence in the rule of law." Sky News separately reports that First Minister Michelle O'Neill has been invited for a police interview over the Garvaghy Road protest.

## 6. Trump strikes a Russian diesel deal with Putin as Zelensky calls it a gift to Moscow

President Donald Trump has announced a deal with Vladimir Putin under which Russia will release 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately "to the American and global marketplace", followed by 500,000 tonnes in November and another million after that, with a further three million tonnes "within a short period" depending on the state of Russian refineries that Ukraine has attacked. The BBC reports the US Treasury is suspending sanctions on Russian diesel exports until 7 April as part of the deal, while other Russian assets in American banks stay frozen. Diesel prices in the US have doubled since the war with Iran began in February, putting pressure on Republicans before the midterms. Putin said Russia, which banned diesel exports earlier this year, was willing to supply the US and global markets, without giving amounts.

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky called the deal a gift to Putin and "an investment in a war that must be ended, not prolonged," saying Russia would repay it with "further terror". The UK said it would "maintain pressure on Russia" through its sanctions regime, and Australia's energy minister Chris Bowen rejected the plan, ABC Australia reports. Hours after the announcement, Ukraine struck a Russian fuel site, according to Al Jazeera.
