Prime Minister Andy Burnham is due to use a speech at the Lord Mayor's banquet at Mansion House to set out three tests that any new agreement with the European Union must pass, ahead of a UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday. The BBC said post-Brexit border check facilities for food would be dismantled under the plan. The Conservatives and Reform have both said they would undo the reset. ## 2. Alan Milburn's youth unemployment review will call for National Insurance cuts for under-25s and an end to some health benefits for young people, ahead of its publication on Tuesday

A review of youth unemployment by former Labour minister Alan Milburn will recommend that employer National Insurance on workers aged 21 to 24 be gradually lowered over five years, starting in retail and hospitality, the BBC reported. The report, due on Tuesday, will suggest doubling the threshold at which businesses start paying NI for that age group from £5,000 to £10,000, but only "when fiscal conditions allow". The Resolution Foundation has estimated that axing NI for 21 to 24-year-olds immediately would cost £5.1bn.

There are nearly one million young people not in employment, education or training. Milburn will recommend that under-25s can no longer claim the health element of universal credit or personal independence payment, replaced by a new youth allowance, alongside a legal guarantee of a job, training or support, with benefits at risk for those who refuse help. He told the BBC that savings to the benefits bill would take 10 years and that he supported subsidising wages for young workers.

Writing in the Guardian, Andy Burnham promised early intervention from childhood and said benefits "must become the backstop, not the starting point". Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden said "benefits are not guaranteed for life", and the Daily Mail reported him saying some young people with conditions such as ADHD or anxiety could receive less in future. Shadow work and pensions secretary Helen Whately said the government should "just stop doing the taxing".

## 6. Saudi-backed Yemeni forces withdraw from positions overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait to defend Aden, leaving the Houthis in control of the key Red Sea waterway

Pro-government forces in Yemen backed by Saudi Arabia said on Sunday they were withdrawing from positions overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait, at the southern end of the Red Sea, leaving the area under the control of the Iran-backed Houthis. The Southern Armed Forces said they were redeploying from Dhubab Airport and the strait area to defend Aden, the interim seat of the internationally recognised government, and neighbouring governorates. Houthi-run media later said the group had taken control of the territory; Saudi Arabia did not comment, the BBC reported.

The Houthis seized much of the coastline along the strait in a surprise offensive in September, squeezing oil exports. Pro-government forces said they had "secured" the strait in early October, but the withdrawal ends a weeks-long effort to retake it. France 24 reported the Houthis had advanced to within about 60 kilometres of Aden. DW said the southern forces also aim to end the Houthi siege of Taiz, a city of nearly two million people where there are fears of famine.

The New York Times quoted the International Crisis Group's Ahmed Nagi saying the Houthis were "very determined to go to Aden". RT noted the strait's importance has grown as traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has been disrupted by the US-Israeli war with Iran.
