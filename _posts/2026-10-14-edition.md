## 1. MI5 chief names Russia the top threat as AI supercharges terror risks

MI5 director general Sir Ken McCallum used his annual threat update to warn that Britain faces more state-backed threats than at any point since the Cold War, with Russia described as the biggest danger and Iran now seeking "harder targets, like military sites", a line widely read as a reference to the RAF Fairford incident. He said artificial intelligence could refine terror plots, supercharge hackers, produce targeted propaganda and even help develop a novel bioweapon. The Guardian reports his warning that years of anti-migrant and anti-Muslim hostility are leading more people to tip "into terrorist intent".

McCallum also confirmed that Russia tried to interfere in the 2016 Brexit referendum through RT, Sputnik and online trolls, but said the attempts were not sophisticated and there was a big difference between what was attempted and what resulted. He warned that prolonging the row would hand the Kremlin a propaganda win. ## 3. Healey targets online marketplaces in a £1bn VAT crackdown

Chancellor John Healey is preparing to make online marketplaces such as Amazon, eBay and Temu responsible for collecting VAT owed by retailers selling on their platforms, according to the Times, which led its front page on the plan. The measure is aimed at foreign sellers that have avoided VAT because of weak rules and enforcement, and could raise up to £1bn for the Treasury. The Guardian reports that platforms would collect VAT at source for all sellers with annual turnover above £90,000, and that the money is expected to fund business-rate cuts for high street shops.

The Budget is due on 28 October. Pressure is building on Healey and Prime Minister Andy Burnham to help struggling small retailers, while the Institute for Fiscal Studies has warned of £35bn in spending pressures. The Treasury said decisions on tax were a matter for fiscal events rather than for comment on speculation. The Sun framed the plan as a crackdown on Amazon and eBay to help the high street; the Independent tied it to a "white van man" Budget.

## 5. UK resists EU pressure to put tariffs on Chinese vehicles

Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds has told the Guardian that the UK will not impose tariffs on Chinese vehicles because of pressure from Brussels, saying any trade defence decision would be "based on the UK interest" and not on other countries' requests. Speaking on a trade trip to India, he said the UK keeps the issue under review but that the British car industry is export-led, and he rejected claims that the UK could become a "back door" into the EU single market, noting there is no major Chinese manufacturer building cars in Britain.

The EU imposed tariffs of up to 45% on Chinese electric cars in 2024 and last week agreed a quota of 350,000 a year on Chinese hybrids. The Guardian says the comments show divisions that could complicate Andy Burnham's push for closer ties before the UK-EU summit in Brussels on 20 November, and notes that Nissan has agreed to look at building cars in northern England for Chery. The Telegraph ran a comment piece arguing Britain must pick a side.

At least 600,000 people across Ukraine lost electricity after overnight Russian missile and drone strikes, President Volodymyr Zelensky said, with critical energy infrastructure hit in Kyiv, the capital region and Kharkiv, days after Donald Trump declared an "energy ceasefire". Ukraine's emergency service said at least three people were killed and 21 injured in Kyiv; Al Jazeera put the national toll at six or more, including deaths in Dnipropetrovsk and Zaporizhzhia.
