## 1. Badenoch trails tax cuts and rules out Reform deal before conference speech

Kemi Badenoch closes the Conservative conference in Birmingham on Wednesday with a speech that, according to extracts released in advance, will cast the next election as "a battle for the soul of this nation" and accuse Prime Minister Andy Burnham's government of wanting to "wind back the clock to failed ideas of the past." The party has already pledged to halve employer National Insurance for workers aged 21 to 24, from 15% to 7.5%, with the standard rate still applying above £50,270. The Conservatives put the cost at £2.3bn a year, to be funded through a "Conservative Savings Plan" due to be published on Wednesday.

The Times reports that Badenoch will also announce a cut to the 40% rate of inheritance tax, funded by welfare reductions. In a BBC interview on Tuesday, Badenoch said she would rather fight another general election than do a deal with Reform UK in a hung parliament, calling Nigel Farage's party "neck deep in scandal." Labour has said the Conservatives "completely failed at every turn" to grow the economy in 14 years in power, and Chancellor John Healey has said the government's promises are "built on the rock of fiscal discipline."

## 2. Husband charged with murder of Norfolk GP

Mark Cain, 45, has been charged with the murder of his wife, Dr Hannah Cain, 45, a GP known professionally as Dr Hannah Terry. Her body was found at the family home in Fern Hill, Dersingham, Norfolk, after police were called by the ambulance service shortly after 2.20am on Sunday 4 October. He was remanded in custody to appear at Norwich Magistrates' Court on Wednesday. Michael Devaney of the Crown Prosecution Service said the decision to prosecute followed a police investigation, adding: "Our thoughts remain with the family of Hannah Cain at this time."

Grimston Medical Centre, where Dr Terry was listed as a member of the practice team, closed temporarily "due to extremely difficult circumstances affecting our practice team." Tributes have been left outside the surgery. ## 3. France suspends high school classes after biggest day of protests

France's prime minister, Sébastien Lecornu, suspended high school lessons for the rest of the week after the largest day yet of protests over teacher shortages, school timetables and crumbling buildings. The interior ministry said more than 250,000 people marched on Tuesday; unions put the figure at 450,000. Police used tear gas in Paris and Marseille and water cannon in Rennes, and the interior ministry reported 488 arrests and 43 members of the security forces injured on the day. Lecornu said the time freed up should be used for "internal democracy" in schools, and ordered ministers to address students' demands by the end of October.

Lecornu told parliament that at least 215 teenagers and 85 school staff had been injured since the protests began, "the heaviest toll in decades paid by the school system," while his office said 715 police officers had been hurt. More than 6,100 people have been arrested since mid-September, most of them minors, and more than 1,800 face prosecution. Lecornu accused the radical-left France Unbowed of seeking to "sow chaos" seven months before the presidential election. President Emmanuel Macron convened ministers on Tuesday evening. US President Donald Trump wrote that "this isn't about schools, this is about Islam wanting to take over a once great Country!"

## 4. Greens' "Zionism is racism" row deepens as Labour rules out ties

The fallout from the Green Party of England and Wales adopting a motion that treats Zionism as racism and backs "a single democratic Palestinian state in all of historic Palestine" widened on Tuesday. Court documents first reported by the Jewish Chronicle show that Lubna Speitan, who proposed the motion, was listed in 2015 as a "close friend" and potential defence witness for Minh Quang Pham, a British national later jailed for 40 years in the US for supporting al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula. There is no suggestion in the documents that she knew of his criminal activities. The Green Party said she holds no official positions and that it "takes all allegations of this nature seriously."

The motion passed at the party's Brighton conference by 1,022 votes to 813, with four of the party's five MPs opposed; leader Zack Polanski did not vote. Israel has barred Polanski from entering the country, and the European Green Party has distanced itself from the policy. Labour chair Bridget Phillipson called the move "shameful," and one Labour minister said they could not foresee the two parties working together. The Welsh Greens' deputy leader, Phil Davies, resigned from the party. Polanski, who is Jewish, has said "antisemitism and criticism of the Israeli state are, and must always be, completely distinct." He stands in Thursday's Holborn and St Pancras by-election.

## 5. Hackers use Asos app to message customers' phones

Asos app users received a push notification at about 10am BST on Tuesday headed "ASOS HACKED" and addressed to the company's data protection officer and IT team. It read: "We have fully compromised the Snowflake instance. Engage with us, or we will leak it," and linked to a Telegram channel run by a group calling itself the Xuanye Group. Asos said it was investigating unauthorised activity involving third-party platforms it uses to communicate with customers and had restricted access to them. It said names and contact details may have been accessed, but it did not believe payment-card information or account passwords were affected.

Asos shares closed down 9.56% in London. Snowflake said it had "found no compromise of the Snowflake platform." The National Cyber Security Centre is offering assistance to Asos, and its chief executive, Richard Horne, said the incident showed how cyber attacks "can have repercussions for individuals much more widely." Asos serves about 17 million customers in more than 150 markets, and app users in Australia, France, Sweden and Ireland also reported receiving the message. The company said it was too early to quantify any impact on trading.
