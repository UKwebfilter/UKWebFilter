## 1. King Charles calls the slave trade a 'shameful chapter' and a 'monstrous injustice' at the Commonwealth summit, but stops short of an apology

King Charles has described the transatlantic slave trade as a "shameful chapter of history" that filled him with "profound sorrow" as he opened the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda, the BBC reported. He said the trade "still casts a shadow" over the Commonwealth, but his address did not contain a formal apology, despite calls for one before the summit.

DW reported that the King spoke of the "monstrous injustice" of the past and of the need to ensure that "evil need not have the last word", while refraining from any talk of reparations. Prime Minister Andy Burnham, also at the summit, has said he is open to a formal apology but has ruled out reparations, and has spoken of debt resets for smaller Commonwealth nations.

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the BBC that the government's willingness to consider an apology would not lead to higher costs for British taxpayers. He said previous governments had a "sweep it under the carpet" attitude and described former Prime Minister Sir Tony Blair's 2007 remarks as "sorry not sorry". The BBC said ministers are beginning a "structured dialogue" with affected countries, with an apology possibly coming around the time the UK hosts the G20 in Manchester in November 2027.

## 3. Seven passenger flights across Europe, including two from the UK, are searched or diverted after suspected bomb threats, with no explosives found

DW reported that a LOT flight from London Heathrow made an unscheduled landing at Warsaw-Modlin airport, which was shut down until around midday, while an SAS flight from Copenhagen was checked at Warsaw Chopin airport. A Ryanair flight from Manchester to Murcia in Spain was also targeted, and a flight from Israel landed in Sofia after a threat received by email. On Monday, a Wizz Air flight from London to Sofia had been redirected to Budapest.

The UK government called the incidents "malicious hoax threats" and said it had "one of the most robust aviation security systems in the world". The BBC said there was officially no link between the threats, but authorities in several countries would co-operate to assess whether this was a co-ordinated attack.
