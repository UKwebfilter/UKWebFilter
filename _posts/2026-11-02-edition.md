## 1. Five teenage boys are seriously injured after an 'unknown group' forced its way into a Halloween party in Hampshire and stabbed them

Five teenage boys were seriously injured after being stabbed at a Halloween party in Chandler's Ford, near Eastleigh in Hampshire, late on Saturday night, police have said. Officers were called to Park Road shortly after 22:30 GMT, the BBC reported, and the injured teenagers, three boys aged 16 and two aged 17, were taken to hospital.

Hampshire and Isle of Wight Police said it believes an "unknown group" gained entry to the party at an address on Park Road before the stabbings. Scene-of-crime restrictions remained in place in Park Road and Valley Road on Sunday, The Guardian said. An eyewitness told the BBC that ambulances and an air ambulance attended, and another said they saw someone running from the house shouting that he had been stabbed and was being chased by two people.

The senior investigating officer, Det Ch Insp Elizabeth Pirie, said officers were working to identify anyone at the party who saw what happened, had filmed footage or could help identify the group. Police have asked for doorbell camera recordings from the area. Eastleigh MP Liz Jarvis said her thoughts were with the victims and their families and urged people not to speculate or share unverified information. No arrests had been reported.

## 4. MPs say the government is making up its China policy 'as it goes along', risking British security and trade with Europe

The UK is making up its policy on China "as it goes along" and risks having its strategy dictated by Washington or Beijing, a report by the Commons business and trade select committee has concluded, The Guardian reported. The committee said the government's failure to form a coherent strategy since 2024 had "combined the worst of both worlds".

The report said the government had promised a strategic, long-term and consistent approach to China in 2024 but "has not delivered on this". It found ministers could neither deliver commercial opportunities for British businesses nor control the country's "increasing exposure to unquantified, managed risk". Committee chair Liam Byrne, a Labour MP, said the approach also risked Britain being "cut off" from big manufacturing contracts with the EU, and that businesses "cannot plan for the future when the government keeps making up its China policy as it goes along".

The MPs acknowledged the benefits of trade, noting that British firms run profitable operations in China and that consumers benefit from low-cost imports such as electric vehicles. The Times reported that the committee called for a ban on Chinese technology in critical infrastructure, and The Telegraph said Prime Minister Andy Burnham was being urged to ban Chinese cars that could spy on Britain.
